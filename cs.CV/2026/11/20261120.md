### What Does the Visual Encoder Buy? Visual Information Gain in Encoder-Free Multimodal Pretraining
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26917v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26917v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 00:05:17+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 00:05:17+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhixuan Ge, Anqi Li, Wei Qiu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Recent scaling studies ask whether vision-language models still need a visual encoder by comparing encoder-free and encoder-based models on the next-token loss of the text in image-text data. This loss can fall without any use of the image, and two runs with the same loss can differ in what they take from it. Visual information gain (VIG) is the loss increase when the image is replaced by a uniform one. It splits exactly into global information, carried by image statistics, and layout information, which shuffling the patches within the image destroys. We apply it to decoders with 158M to 1.04B parameters that read pixel patches through a linear embedding, evaluated every billion tokens up to 40B, and to 19 public checkpoints. In our decoders the image lowers the loss by 3% to 46%, depending on the image type. Global information is present after the first billion tokens. Layout information starts near zero, grows slowly with training and does not grow with model size on five of six image types, and a larger batch reaches the same loss with less of it. With a frozen SigLIP 2 encoder in front of the same decoder the loss is lower only when the arrangement of the image is intact. On shuffled images the losses with and without the encoder agree to within 0.04 nats on five of six image types, so the net advantage of the encoder in the loss is layout information, above all the ability to read text in images. Public encoder-free models at production scale (Gemma 4 12B, NEO, Mono-InternVL) do acquire layout information. On the same documents they have 0.53 to 0.76 nats of it, against 0.65 to 0.82 for recent encoder-based models and 0.04 to 0.07 for our decoders, and their released training stages show it rising. These results agree with the reported parity of the two families when both are trained from scratch and place the reported advantage of a pretrained encoder in layout information.



### Tokenize Moments, Not Frames: Tokenizer-Aware Budget Allocation for Training-Free Video LLM Acceleration
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26933v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26933v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 00:34:57+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 00:34:57+00:00
- **Authors**: Zirui Zhao, Kai Liu, Linghe Kong
- **Comment**: 24 pages, 9 figures, 27 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Video large language models (LLMs) represent each video with thousands of visual tokens, and training-free accelerators that prune or merge these tokens lose accuracy on long videos. Analyzing frame similarity and the vision tokenizers of 16 open models, we trace this loss to a step before token selection. Under a token budget, the frames of a long video are sampled farther apart than the roughly 2 s over which frames remain similar. The two-frame 3D patches of models such as Qwen-VL then blend two unrelated scenes into each visual token, a double exposure that arises before any pruning or merging. Fixed-size tokenizers such as InternVL's avoid this blend but respond differently to the same reduction. We propose ESSENCE, a training-free method that tokenizes moments, segments within which frames remain similar, rather than frames. Before encoding, a closed-form rule uses the sampling interval and the tokenizer class to set the number of token grids and the dimension to reduce; visual tokens are then released from later LLM layers. Against five recent accelerators on Qwen3-VL, Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3.5, ESSENCE achieves higher accuracy in 58 of 60 comparisons at matched mean time to first token (TTFT). With 80% of the tokens, it matches the average accuracy of the full Qwen models at $1.6\times$ faster TTFT; on GLM-4.1V, it exceeds the full model by up to 3.4 points. Code is available at: https://github.com/zhkandsrz/ESSENCE.

### SecretAgent: A Multi-Agent Framework for Privacy Protection against Vision Language Models
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26936v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI, cs.CR, cs.LG, cs.MA
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26936v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 00:46:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Minda Zhao, Yue Zhang, Mengyu Wang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Vision-language models (VLMs) can infer private information from shared images, including locations, personally identifiable information (PII), and personal attributes such as age and sex, even from medical images. Adversarial perturbations can protect such information against black-box VLMs, but existing methods overlook image quality and introduce visible artifacts into the images they protect. We present SecretAgent, the first general multi-agent framework that protects image privacy against VLMs while preserving visual fidelity. LLM agents iteratively locate the visual evidence that reveals private information and adapt an invisible perturbation to the target VLM's answers and to an appearance reviewer agent, which rejects any candidate with visible artifacts. We instantiate SecretAgent with two complementary variants: SecretAgent-Gradient optimizes the perturbation through six surrogate encoders for cross-model transferability, and the gradient-free SecretAgent-Tool composes procedural noise and achieves the best perceptual fidelity on three of the five datasets. Experiments on four privacy applications (geolocation, PII, facial, and medical) across five datasets and six VLMs show that SecretAgent preserves image quality substantially better than existing methods while achieving state-of-the-art privacy protection in every application, and that its protection transfers to other VLMs.

### Serial Dependence Emerges from Optimizing Vision for Noise Robustness
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26949v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **q-bio.NC**, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26949v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 01:00:28+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 01:00:28+00:00
- **Authors**: Hiroshi Higashi, Keigo Sugihara, Yuichi Tanaka
- **Comment**: 20 pages, 10 figures, 1 table. Code and data: https://github.com/labbip/noise_sd
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Human visual perception is dynamically shaped by the recent past, a phenomenon known as serial dependence. Mechanistic models show that a residual memory trace can generate this dependency, yet its functional purpose (why the visual system should carry information forward in time at all) remains debated. We tested the hypothesis that serial dependence emerges as a normative consequence of optimizing vision for robustness against sensory noise. We trained recurrent autoencoders (a CNN encoder, a ConvGRU memory module, and a CNN decoder) to reconstruct natural videos corrupted by Gaussian noise, with no objective referring to perceptual history. Models that used their temporal memory achieved lower reconstruction error than memory-free counterparts, and this advantage widened as training noise increased, indicating that integrating past context is an adaptive denoising strategy. Probing the trained models with Gabor and dot sequences, we found that memory gave rise to serial dependence spontaneously: an attractive bias toward previous orientations and a spatial "continuity field" toward recently seen locations, both strengthening with training noise. Across temporal delays, the model's serial dependence was strongest for the immediately preceding stimulus and decayed monotonically. This profile quantitatively matched human orientation judgments, including an independent public dataset, once a known response-autocorrelation confound was controlled: an apparent non-monotonicity in one dataset reflected this measurement artifact rather than a genuine model-human mismatch. These findings suggest that serial dependence need not be an idiosyncratic quirk but can arise as a byproduct of optimizing for noise robustness, offering a normative account of perceptual history-dependence that complements existing mechanistic explanations and links behavior to an underlying computational objective.



### Learning Video Foresight Reasoning from Unlabeled Videos via On-Policy Self-Distillation

- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26954v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 01:08:12+00:00

- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Video foresight reasoning requires vision-language models to anticipate future events or next actions from currently observed videos. Existing methods fit future captions, rationales, or full visual states, but face two limitations. First, they rely on annotations or external large models for supervision, limiting scalability. We address this with an on-policy self-distillation framework that turns past-future video pairs into a natural self-supervised signal without extra annotations or LLM supervision. Second, they treat the whole future clip as equally valuable, although most of it is irrelevant context information. We call the resulting framework Future-OPSD. ### Geo-Force: A Theory of Force Sensing from Object Geometry
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26992v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.RO**, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26992v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 02:23:31+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 02:23:31+00:00
- **Authors**: Abdullah Ayad
- **Comment**: 17 pages, 14 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Robots need contact forces to handle delicate objects, but tactile sensors are fragile and hard to integrate, whereas depth sensors, such as RGB-D cameras, are widely available. A virtual touch sensor that keeps the sensing surface of a tactile sensor but replaces its electronics with a touch signal predicted from the target object's geometry, as measured by a depth sensor, could close this gap, yet it is unclear what such a virtual sensor computes and when its output can be trusted. We give a formal answer. We define the Geometric Force as the force on each taxel of a virtual touch sensor, computed in three steps: rendering the surface to be touched as seen from the virtual sensor, predicting the touch signal with a touch map, learned or simulated, and estimating the force from that signal. We prove three properties of the Geometric Force. First, its force vectors only rotate under a change of coordinate frame. Second, it vanishes when the rendered surface does not touch the virtual sensor. Third, it is stable: its error grows at most linearly with the errors of the three steps for Lipschitz maps. The rendering error, in turn, depends on how well the surface is sampled or reconstructed and how accurately the sensor is placed. We then show a fundamental limit: geometry does not reveal the object's stiffness, so no touch map can predict touch exactly for objects of the same shape and different stiffness. Under an elastic contact model, all object-specific knowledge resides in the touch map. From these results, we derive requirements on the depth sensor and the objects, design rules, and the conditions under which such a virtual sensor fails.
