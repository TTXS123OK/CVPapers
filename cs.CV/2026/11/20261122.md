- **Comment**: 17 pages, 13 tables, 4 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: As multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) get increasingly used as collaborative partners across embodied robotics, AR/VR, and multi-user systems, spatial reasoning is now often distributed across agents with partial environment visibility, necessitating spatial reasoning integration. Prior work in language-mediated spatial integration is however strictly limited to simplified two-agent scenarios. To address this gap, we first formalize the general problem of decentralized multi-view spatial reasoning, where N agents observe distinct viewpoints, exchange budget-constrained natural language messages, and collaborate to enable a listener to answer spatial queries. Nevertheless, we observe that substantial headroom still remains for multi-agent spatial reasoning in this setting, as evidenced by a significant gap between current centralized performance and an oracle best-of-K ceiling. Moreover, we show that reinforcement learning with credit assignment substantially bolsters the decentralized model, effectively closing the gap with the centralized baseline.

- **Summary**: A Compton camera measures two events in a detector from individual gamma-rays to reconstruct the radiation source. Conventional scintillator-based Compton cameras consist of two separate scintillator volumes or a single segmented scintillator, resulting in drawbacks regarding detector bulkiness and efficiency. Their scintillator readout techniques struggle to resolve two nearby events in one monolithic scintillator. Single-photon avalanche diode (SPAD) cameras have recently been proposed for measuring scintillation events in various configurations. Our approach is able to resolve two simultaneous events in one monolithic scintillator and enables Compton camera devices with compact, monolithic scintillator volumes for enhanced portability.

- **Summary**: Diagnosing sarcopenic dysphagia typically requires costly supplementary imaging such as CT or MRI to assess muscle atrophy, burdening elderly patients. ### Supply the Global, Supervise the Local: Combining Representation Conditioning and Alignment in Diffusion Transformers
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29138v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29138v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-22 01:45:42+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-22 01:45:42+00:00
- **Authors**: Chaojie Ji, Sergio Quiroga-Sandoval, Ruxin Wang, Zheju Zheng, Yankai Cao
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: A pretrained image representation helps train a diffusion transformer in several roles, including as an alignment target, which the model's hidden states learn to match, as in representation alignment (REPA), and as a condition, an extra input describing the image to be generated. Combined, they would give the model an input and a target, but whether they conflict is unknown. To test this, we let the condition carry only part of REPA's target: we split the representation of each image patch into an image-level (global) part, shared by all patches of the image, and a within-image (local) part, the patch's difference from the image-level part, and use one token carrying the image-level part as the condition. Then, the two roles overlap on the image-level part, where they could conflict, while only REPA supervises the within-image part, where we can see whether the token helps REPA.   We find that the token and REPA together teach both the image-level part and the within-image part, the main source of REPA's FID improvement, far more than the sum of what each teaches alone, so they cooperate rather than conflict. These findings give one design rule for combining REPA with an image-level condition, supply the global, supervise the local, which we implement as Global-Condition REPA (GC-REPA): REPA with the token as an extra input; the token carries no information beyond REPA's target and is drawn at sampling from a separately trained token prior. GC-REPA greatly accelerates convergence: on ImageNet 256*256, SiT-XL/2 + GC-REPA reaches REPA's 4M- and SiT-XL/2's 7M-iteration FIDs within 200K and 100K iterations (20 and 70 fewer).



### RAVEC: Reward-Aligned Visual Evidence Correction

- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29142v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-22 01:50:20+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-22 01:50:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Junjie Yang, Rui Qian, Xutao Mao, Yuhe Wu, Mingda Zhang, Jiaxuan Luo, Yang Tan, Qiang Huang, Zhisheng Chen, Yanfeng Li, Dawen Tian, Changmiao Wang, Tao Tan
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards improves multimodal reasoning, but response-level feedback provides limited guidance on which intermediate decisions to correct. Visual interventions reveal sensitivity to image content, yet this sensitivity alone does not establish reward alignment or support across questions. We introduce RAVEC (Reward-Aligned Visual Evidence Correction), which selects visual corrections using local and cross-question reward support and jointly determines their strengths within each response. Paired Visual Evidence Contrast (PVEC) compares predictions from an exponential moving average teacher at the same sampled response prefix under the original image and a version with its annotated evidence region suppressed, yielding signed correction directions. Credit-Risk Allocation (CRA) screens these directions using local reward alignment and reward support from other questions, excluding all responses to the source question. It then solves a response-level budgeted allocation problem, assigning additional cost to directions that locally reduce predictive entropy. The resulting signed visual loss augments Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) during training. Inference uses only the original image and question. Across ten multimodal benchmarks, RAVEC achieves the highest average accuracy among the compared methods with both 3B and 7B Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct models, outperforming GRPO by 2.67 and 1.39 percentage points, respectively. Component ablations support the combined design of evidence alignment, credit qualification, and risk-aware allocation. The project is available at https://yangjj007.github.io/ravec/.

- **Authors**: Savya Khosla, Chris Dongjoo Kim, Alex Schwing, Derek Hoiem, Christopher Clark, Sangho Lee
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) tokenize images using fixed spatial grids, so visual token count grows with pixel area rather than scene content. We propose to group neighboring patches based on their visual appearance, allowing tokens to represent coherent image regions rather than fixed spatial windows. We introduce RVLM, a VLM trained to use regions rather than patches as its visual units. Analyzing region construction for general-purpose VLMs, we find that regions must cover the full image, stay spatially connected during end-to-end training, and preserve fine structure such as small text, while semantic grouping adds little. The gains are largest on spatially demanding tasks, where prior token-reduction methods degrade most sharply. RVLM adds no model parameters and improves inference throughput by up to 35.9%. On unseen pathology and satellite images and on video benchmarks without any video training, RVLM performs on par with the patch baseline while using 2-3$\times$ fewer visual tokens. These results establish regions as a scalable alternative to patch-based visual representations while preserving the spatial precision required for grounded prediction.

- **Summary**: Terrestrial laser scanning (TLS) enables 3D reconstruction of trees and quantitative structure models (QSMs) for estimating woody volume and structural traits. - **Summary**: Background: Deep learning fundus classifiers often perform well internally, but duplicated images, inconsistent labels, acquisition differences and poor threshold transfer can undermine their reliability. - **Summary**: Recent geometric foundation models can successfully convert a video sequence into frames of registered point clouds, but cannot predict moments beyond the observed temporal resolution. We propose a continuous-time 4D reconstruction method that extracts 3D structure and camera poses at any time instant, with additional guidance from event-camera measurements. While event cameras provide evidence at microsecond resolution to continuously fill the temporal gap, integrating them into existing RGB-based foundation models remains challenging. We formulate the problem as interpolation in the latent feature space and augment event-based losses in the image space as an auxiliary task to boost accuracy. Operating in the latent space leverages the powerful geometric priors of foundation models, while the event loss provides physically grounded guidance. To effectively address the lack of paired training data, we further propose a self-supervised training framework that uses pseudo-ground-truth labels of estimated geometry and simulated events generated from high-frame-rate RGB videos. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate that the proposed pipeline outperforms baselines that temporally interpolate either the input RGB videos or the estimated geometry, and that it generalizes to unseen real-world scenarios. Controlled experiments with synthetic corruptions show that it remains robust under motion-blur and low-light conditions.



### Dynamics Forcing: Mitigating Error Accumulation in Causal Video World Models

- **Authors**: Xin Zhou, Cong Miao
- **Comment**: 9 pages, 5 figures, 3 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Causal video world models generate block by block from their own previous outputs. Small errors in early blocks are fed back as history and grow until the scene and the robot fall apart. Teacher forcing trains on clean ground-truth history, so the model never sees the history it will actually condition on, and it also finds a shortcut: copying the previous frame instead of predicting how the scene changes. We present Dynamics Forcing, a training scheme that keeps real video as the only supervision and reshapes how history is presented. History is perturbed in three ways: random noising, self-resampling by the model itself, and joint denoising, in which the most recent blocks are still partly noisy when the next block is predicted. On top of this we move history guidance and first-frame anchor guidance from inference into training, so that the model is pushed to use its history and its reference frame rather than ignore them. Dynamics Forcing needs no teacher model, no distillation, and no additional model at inference. Applied to a 5B video model with LoRA on 77 hours of manipulation video on a single GPU, it better matches unseen real continuations and reduces error accumulation.
