### Correctness Metric Matters: NWD-Based Calibration Loss for Tiny Person Detection in UAV Infrared Imagery
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11735v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11735v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-09 01:19:22+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-09 01:19:22+00:00
- **Authors**: Tian Zhang, Zhirong Su
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Detection calibration aligns predicted confidence with empirical correctness. Existing calibration losses inherit their correctness definition from box regression, specifically IoU against a hard threshold. We show that this target is geometrically ill-posed for tiny objects in UAV infrared imagery, where persons occupy less than 0.1\% of the image. The same pixel error is penalized more heavily as the target shrinks, so binary prediction calibration (BPC) actively suppresses confidences that should be high. On HIT-UAV, BPC with an IoU target degrades image-level localization-aware ECE (LaECE) from 0.0689 to 0.0761, 0.0649 to 0.0733, and 0.0653 to 0.0682 across Low, Mid, and High signal-to-clutter ratio (SCR) groups. We replace the IoU target with a continuous, scale-invariant normalized Wasserstein distance (NWD). The NWD target reduces LaECE\(_{\mathrm{NWD}}\) relative to BPC+IoU on all three groups (\(p<10^{-7}\)) with mAP neutral (0.9356 vs.\ 0.9347), and an independent center-offset anchor confirms that the gain is a genuine geometric correction. The improvement holds across a broad plateau of calibration weights. The NWD formulation requires no inference-time mapping layer. If a validation-fitted isotonic map is applied on top of it, calibration is lowered further, and the two corrections act on different components of the same error. The metric-level remedy has a measurable boundary. Target-background thermal pairing in the training distribution and detection-level aggregation under per-image detection-count heterogeneity lie outside it. Both are quantified independently of the NWD target.



### Dense Flow from Adaptive Correspondence
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11738v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11738v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-09 01:26:43+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-09 01:26:43+00:00
- **Authors**: Cheng-Lin Hsieh, Stella X. Yu
- **Comment**: 18 pages, 6 figures. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Optical flow requires reliable matching from sufficient visual context and precise motion at every pixel. Existing methods such as WAFT match and estimate flow on the same coarse grids, then recover dense flow through learned upsampling, without explicitly linking visual support for matching to dense flow reconstruction. Our key insight is that dense pixel-level flow does not require equally dense matching. We decouple correspondence scale from flow resolution by adapting the size and shape of spatial supports to image structure for reliable matching, while reusing these supports for precise dense flow reconstruction. We realize this in Flow from Adaptive Token Estimation (FATE), whose token supports expand within coherent regions and contract near motion boundaries. Shared pixel-to-token associations pool features for matching and reconstruct pixel flow from token motions. Flow supervision jointly trains pixel grouping, token matching, and flow estimation without grouping labels, allowing FATE to anticipate which pixels are likely to move together from appearance alone. Trained on FlyingChairs/FlyingThings3D, FATE achieves the highest zero-shot accuracy on Sintel Final and KITTI, a critical setting given scarce real-world flow labels. For visible pixels, FATE improves accuracy within coherent regions, and more near motion boundaries. Its largest gains occur where direct matches are unavailable: Occluded or out-of-frame pixels account for roughly 10% of valid pixels but 50% of WAFT's total error. Experiments show that adaptive correspondence through pixel-to-token grouping enables reliable matching and precise pixel-level flow, improving generalization to unseen real scenes.

- **Summary**: Compact object detectors such as YOLOv8n are a common choice for camera-based fire and smoke detection on low-power devices. These detectors are usually evaluated with box-level metrics such as mean average precision, and their outputs are turned into decisions with a fixed confidence threshold such as 0.25 or 0.5. A fire monitoring system, however, makes one decision per image: raise an alarm or not. In this paper we evaluate two publicly available compact detectors on the D-Fire test set at the image level. - **Authors**: Reyhaneh Ahani Manghotay, Jie Liang

- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Monocular depth estimation has advanced through large-scale pretraining and strong visual backbones, yet metric depth remains sensitive to scene-level scale. Indoor rooms and outdoor driving scenes occupy very different depth ranges, but most depth decoders infer this global structure implicitly from visual features. We ask whether scene-level semantic structure can instead act as an upstream prior that shapes the metric output space before dense prediction.   We present LangDepth (SemanticBins), a monocular depth architecture that couples strong visual features with a scene-conditioned output space. A frozen CLIP encoder and a learnable bank of scene- and geometry-oriented concept keys convert image features into a compact scene descriptor, without requiring per-image text or an external captioner. A Scene Prior Module (SPM) maps this descriptor to a scene-conditioned metric depth interval, providing a global range anchor before dense decoding. In parallel, a DINOv2-L backbone with a lightweight FPN extracts dense visual features, and a Per-Pixel Assignment Network (PPAN) assigns pixels to the scene-prescribed bin structure. A confidence gate fuses the dense visual estimate with the SPM prior.   We train LangDepth jointly on NYU Depth V2 and KITTI under a modest supervised depth budget, without web-scale depth supervision or pseudo-labelled depth data. A single model achieves delta_1 = 0.960 on NYU, 0.960 on KITTI, 0.887 on DIODE indoor, 0.946 on iBims-1, and 0.861 on SUN RGB-D. Beyond aggregate accuracy, the SPM predicts compact indoor ranges and much wider outdoor KITTI ranges, the gate becomes SPM-dominant on outdoor scenes, and perturbing the routed scene context causes large drops on KITTI and iBims-1. These results support scene-conditioned output spaces as a practical and interpretable mechanism for cross-domain monocular depth estimation.

- **Summary**: Global structure-from-motion (SfM) commonly refines relative poses on individual image pairs and then estimates global rotations by rotation averaging.
