### CHROMA-MILL: Joint Low-Light Enhancement and White Balance under Colored Illumination
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04092v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04092v1)

- **Authors**: Pol Riubrogent-Comas, David Serrano-Lozano, Maria Pilligua, Michael S. Brown, Ramon Baldrich, Javier Vazquez-Corral
- **Comment**: Accepted at WACV 2027. 16 pages (main paper + supplementary material), 7 figures, 7 tables. Project page: https://color.cvc.uab.cat/chromamill/
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Capturing an image in a low-light environment produces a weak, noisy sensor signal. Low-light image enhancement (LLIE) aims to restore it. Auto white balance (AWB) is also required for every image, and the same noisy signal degrades its ability to infer the scene illuminant. LLIE is typically performed first, followed by AWB, but this strategy remains largely unexamined because existing low-light datasets span a narrow range of illuminants and lack illuminant ground truth for evaluating AWB. We address this with CHROMA-MILL, a RAW dataset of 50 scenes captured with a DSLR and smartphone under four calibrated illuminants at eleven intensity levels. Because light is additive, we blend these captures in the linear RAW domain to synthesize physically valid illuminant mixtures with exact chromaticity ground truth. Benchmarking on this data, we find joint correction is best, and introduce CTI-Retinexformer, which supervises an illumination map, a chromaticity prediction, and an illumination-invariant scene embedding, outperforming the best cascade by 0.7 dB PSNR$_L$ and 1.1 $ΔE_{00}$.

### Co-Occurrence Priors Override Visual Spatial Evidence: Understanding False Alarms in Vision-Language Models for Construction Struck-By Hazard Detection

- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04112v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-11-03 00:39:35+00:00
- **Authors**: Xiaoshan Zhou
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Vision-language models (VLMs) increasingly support construction hazard monitoring, yet their frequent false alarms remain poorly understood. This study investigates whether false struck-by hazard warnings arise from failures in object recognition, spatial relation encoding, or hazard decisions dominated by learned co-occurrence priors. We evaluated Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL3.5-8B using 355 counterfactual worker-excavator scene pairs with controlled spatial proximity and worker-removal variants, combining behavioral analysis, internal representation probing, causal interventions, and decision correction. Both models recognized the entities and encoded their spatial relations (out-of-fold AUROC: 0.82 and 0.91), assigning higher hazard scores to near than far scenes in 79% and 73% of pairs. Nevertheless, 74% and 100% of far scenes were falsely classified as hazardous. Removing the worker reduced Qwen's hazard rate to 11%, while worker presence exerted 2.4- and 3.2-times greater influence on hazard scores than spatial proximity in the respective models. Attention-head interventions, neuron-level interventions, and activation steering failed to eliminate this co-occurrence bias. However, conditioning hazard decisions on the model's own spatial-relation representation improved Qwen's specificity from 0.36 to 0.58 at matched sensitivity, outperforming threshold recalibration. These findings reveal that VLM struck-by false alarms stem primarily from decision-making failures rather than deficiencies in object recognition or spatial encoding, suggesting that future efforts should prioritize conditioning hazard decisions on encoded spatial evidence rather than further improving visual recognition alone.



### An automated photometric pipeline for measuring the corotation radius of spiral galaxies
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04123v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **astro-ph.GA**, cs.CV, physics.data-an
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04123v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-03 00:55:18+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-03 00:55:18+00:00
- **Authors**: Kimia Yazdani, Sahel Mesforoush, Wayne B. Hayes
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: The corotation radius R_CR of a spiral galaxy marks the radius where the angular speed of the spiral pattern matches the orbital speed of the disk material. Despite its importance for understanding spiral structure and galaxy evolution, very few galaxies have corotation measurements, often derived using heterogeneous techniques. We present an automated photometric pipeline that identifies corotation radii by tracking the azimuthal phase offset between young and old stellar populations. Building on the color-gradient framework of \citet{Puerari1997}, we extract azimuthal brightness profiles from deprojected dual-band images, fit periodic models to the profiles, and identify corotation candidates at radii where the phase difference between the two stellar tracers changes sign. Each candidate is assigned an adaptive uncertainty interval and reported with a goodness-of-fit metric. We benchmark the method against a cross-method literature compilation, scoring every technique -- including our own -- against a reference built from the published measurements alone and evaluated leave-one-out. Over the $90$ galaxies for which such a reference exists, the pipeline places a correct corotation radius in $80.0\%$ of them, recovers $63.6\%$ of their known corotation radii, and reaches a harmonic mean of recall and purity of $0.58$, the highest values of any method in the comparison. Unlike the published methods, each of which was applied to a small, curated set of galaxies, our pipeline is run on the entire reference sample without any pre-selection, a sample two to six times larger than existing methods. The pipeline is scalable to large surveys, flexible with respect to spiral mode, and naturally recovers multiple corotation radii in galaxies with complex spiral structure, providing a uniform framework for studying disk resonances across diverse galaxy populations.

- **Comment**: None

- **Summary**: Long-horizon video generation is increasingly moving toward autoregressive rollout, where each newly generated segment becomes part of the evidence used for future prediction. Recent causal decoders, long-context training, and KV-cache mechanisms have extended feasible duration, but most inference pipelines still decide the visible past implicitly through recent windows, compressed caches, or external memory. This raises a direct question: \textbf{can the choice of which past frames or segments to show to the decoder be treated as an explicit test-time decision?} We answer this question with \textsc{ReCR}, a training-free method that formulates autoregressive video decoding as representation-guided context rewriting. - **Summary**: Reasoning vision-language-action (VLA) models pair physical control with natural-language rationales that supervisors can read to monitor embodied safety.
