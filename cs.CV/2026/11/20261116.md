### Words Speak Louder? Exploring the Language Shortcut of LLM-Driven Incomplete Multimodal Learning
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20349v2
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.MM
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20349v2)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 00:25:33+00:00

- **Authors**: Qiangchang Wang, Chong Xiao, Xiaokun Bai, Rundong He, Zhuang Qi, Xusheng Liang, Zhibin Wu, Wenhao Li
- **Comment**: 19 pages, 7 figures, 14 tables. Project page: https://peacelwh.github.io/CoVeR/
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Is a face or a voice still heard once the words are known? Incomplete multimodal learning increasingly uses large language models (LLMs) as fusion engines: modality features enter as token blocks and missing modalities become placeholders. We find that LLM-driven fusion can override non-verbal evidence it can read. Contrasting its full-input prediction with counterfactual views, the same model shown one modality or no content, we identify language shortcuts. We build MR-Bench: MR-Bench-A types every test sample of four sentiment benchmarks; MR-Bench-H contains 900 human-validated clips (620 language-shortcut, LS; 280 non-verbal-shortcut, NS) from seven sources, and every model is scored on all of them. Zero-shot MLLMs solve at most 32.7% of the LS clips; adding the transcript to the muted video lowers vision-language accuracy by 46.1-53.1 points. Yet inverting a text-only model's answers solves 94.4%, so LS accuracy alone cannot establish use of non-verbal evidence. Inside the fusion LLM, fused states align increasingly with the text view late in the decoder. Counterfactual-View Reliability learning (CoVeR) trains views as unimodal judges and withholds fused supervision where they disagree. It achieves state-of-the-art results on five benchmarks without extra inference cost in accuracy mode. On MR-Bench-H, it reaches 61.7% LS and 93.6% NS accuracy; robust mode raises LS accuracy to 63.0% while lowering NS accuracy to 84.8%. Among LS clips solved without text, adding text causes errors on 30.1% with CoVeR versus 32.2% with LLM-Fusion. Project page: https://peacelwh.github.io/CoVeR/.



### BlockAlign: Preference Optimization for Trajectory-Distilled Video Generation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20351v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20351v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 00:28:08+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-16 00:28:08+00:00
- **Authors**: Jingyuan Zhu, Biaolong Chen, Yangyang Qin, Shengyang Shen, Yaohua Wang, Weiming Bai, Miao Lu, Aixi Zhang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Directly aligning trajectory-distilled video generators slashes rollout costs and optimizes the deployed few-step model without teacher fine-tuning or re-distillation. However, conventional flow-matching preference objectives assume single-head local velocity supervision, mismatching the finite-interval, block-average transition interface of trajectory-distilled generators such as Parallel Decoding Distillation (PDD) and Parametric Trajectory Distillation (PTD). Naively supervising only the primary initial-subinterval velocity sub-head rather than the fused block-average velocity at block starts (DirectAlign) induces a severe training--inference mismatch that degrades quality below the unaligned baseline. We introduce BlockAlign, an interface-consistent preference optimization framework that constructs states at valid block boundaries, queries the fused block-level velocity predictor, and supervises span-normalized transition residuals-seamlessly supporting DPO-, NFT-, and rollout-style objectives while preserving the native few-step sampler. We further build a unified post-training pipeline integrating offline pairs, reward-free semi-online continuations, and online rollouts with hierarchical preference selection and objective-aware block scheduling. Experiments on multi-reference video generation across MiniMax-H3 Ref2VA and an in-house model RefVid-V4 show that BlockAlign consistently outperforms unaligned distilled models and DirectAlign in structural plausibility, reference consistency, and visual quality. Notably, our approach unlocks PDD's 5x few-step rollout speedup for 3x faster end-to-end online training, achieving statistically significant human preference gains over PDD baselines (54.0% vs. 33.3% wins/losses, or 61.8% non-tie win rate on RefVid-V4; 51.3% vs. 34.7%, or 59.7% non-tie win rate on MiniMax-H3) and up to an 82.7% win rate against DirectAlign.

- **Summary**: Existing multi-granularity methods for monocular 3D human pose estimation emphasize spatial joint features while underusing skeletal structure, including global connectivity and temporal continuity. Moreover, modeling entire frame sequences incurs high computational costs. We propose a Structure-Aware Multi-Granularity Spatiotemporal Transformer that integrates structural priors across spatial and temporal representations for accurate and efficient pose estimation. A three-level feature extractor captures human topology at the joint, bone, and limb levels. Experiments demonstrate improved accuracy and efficiency over conventional Transformer-based approaches, highlighting the effectiveness of structure-aware multi-granularity modeling for monocular 3D human pose estimation.

### DominoBench: Evaluating Multi-Step Physical Reasoning from Still Images
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20385v2
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20385v2)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 01:24:42+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-17 07:40:39+00:00
- **Authors**: Emilio Ferrara
- **Comment**: DominoBench dataset, evaluation harnesses, and benchmarks available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/emilioferrara/DominoBench
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Can a vision-language model look at a picture of a chain reaction and tell what will happen? Recent multimodal models describe scenes fluently and solve textbook physics problems, which suggests they should also predict how a scene of ramps, ledges, pendulums, seesaws and dominoes plays out once a ball is pushed. We tested this claim on 30 models from 10 families and reached a clear conclusion: unaided, no model predicts the outcome reliably. We built DominoBench, a benchmark of still images of 2D rigid-body scenes whose outcome a physics simulator computes. It holds 854 items in 427 minimal pairs, in which a change of one to three grid steps in one quantity decides whether one person survives, and every item comes with a four-rung hint ladder ending in the exact calculation that decides the outcome. Unaided, frontier models from Anthropic, Google and OpenAI are right about the person whose fate flips in at most 75% of items and answer both twins of a pair correctly in at most 40%. Pointing at the deciding step helps little, and exact geometry given as text barely helps, yet once the deciding calculation and the quantities it needs, including speeds that a still image cannot show, are written out, the same models reach 80-92% on the deciding person, while small open-weight models stay near chance. Physical prediction from a still image therefore fails at the quantitative step: the strongest models use the deciding quantity when they are given it, but do not produce it on their own. For models that plan or act in the physical world, a fluent account of a scene is thus no evidence of a correct prediction: such models must find and compute the deciding quantity unprompted, and DominoBench tests whether they do.

- **Authors**: Wei Qin Chuah, Zeji Hui, Amirali Khodadadian Gostar, Alireza Bab-Hadiashar
- **Comment**: 22 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Scene chronology aims to reconstruct a scene across visits separated by hours or days, during which objects may change in position, presence or shape. Such reconstruction must retain every earlier state, yet each revisit often provides only a few images. Storing a complete model for each visit duplicates the unchanged scene, whereas updating a single shared model alters earlier states. We present ReScene, which stores each visit as an immutable compact update, so that any earlier visit can be recovered exactly by applying the stored updates in order, without retaining past images. To localise each update, ReScene attributes the disagreement between the preceding reconstruction and the new images to individual Gaussians through their compositing weights. This attribution guides the suppression of outdated Gaussians and the refinement of persistent ones, while newly visible surfaces are initialised from learned multi-view depth. We also introduce Multi-State, a benchmark of non-rigid deformation and partial material removal, including synthetic scenes with ground-truth geometry. ReScene achieves quality higher than sequential 3DGS checkpointing with 2.5--4.8$\times$ less uncompressed history storage. At comparable PSNR, it also reduces foreground depth error by 3.2$\times$ relative to sequential ChronoGS, showing that accurate rendering does not necessarily imply accurate geometry. Code and benchmark are available at https://waychin-weiqin.github.io/ReScene/.

- **Summary**: Text-to-image (T2I) self-correction combines a vision-language model (VLM) critic that diagnoses errors and proposes instructions with an image editor that executes them. We first learn a Bradley-Terry quality model from pairwise human judgments. A perception head then regresses spherical-harmonic lighting from frozen self-supervised features via cross-attention. Finally, a placement solver isolates the light via spherical-harmonic linearity and optimizes its reachable position. On held-out environments, LightMatch attains 87.8% of the oracle improvement, outperforming the conventional solver that ignores ambient light by a relative 28.4% and the same pipeline with the widely used DPR estimator by a relative 70.5%. It reaches 73.2% on real OLAT faces of unseen identities and, on our real captures, cuts lamp-direction error by 37.7%.
