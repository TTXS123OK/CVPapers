### Hard or Mislabeled? Failure Screens Enrich Vision Benchmarks for Label Errors
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19044v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19044v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 00:38:20+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 00:38:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Haolin Guo
- **Comment**: 28 pages, 5 figures, 16 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Hard vision benchmarks often keep images that strong classifiers get wrong. Since a classifier is also marked wrong when the label is wrong, such failure screens may collect label errors. We measure this on ImageNet's twice-relabeled validation set with 103 classifiers. Among the 1,892 images that all 66 supervised classifiers get wrong, 61% have a wrong label, versus 11.1% overall. The fewer images a screen lets through, the more of them are mislabeled, along approximately one curve. Fit on one model family, this curve predicts the screens of two held-out model families with a mean error of 2.4 percentage points, and replays of ImageNet-D's and ImageNet-Hard's screens within 0.1 and 6.3 points. On new pools, ImageNet-V2 and CIFAR-10, enrichment at the strictest screens is stronger than the curve predicts: on ImageNet-V2, by human relabeling, 67% of the images that all 66 classifiers get wrong are mislabeled, against 46% predicted. Screens that let models choose among only some of the classes, as ImageNet-A's does (200 of 1,000), fall below the curve. By ReImageNet, 32% of ImageNet-Hard's ImageNet images have no correct label. ScreenAudit, which comprises an audit tool, a script that rebuilds corrected answer sets for the 5,464 decided ImageNet-Hard images (of 5,646) from ReImageNet's gated release, and per-image audits of 1,198 benchmark images, will be released later.



### SearchChord: Prompt Preselection and Field Recomposition for One-Step Image Editing
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19076v1
- **DOI**: None

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19076v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 01:26:24+00:00
- **Authors**: Boyang Deng, Yuzhen Lu
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 3 figures, conference
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Finding effective prompts and seeds for one-step image editors requires balancing edit fidelity, source preservation, and computational cost. To address this challenge, we explore a large collection of candidate edits, where each candidate is defined by a unique prompt-seed pair, and generate corresponding images on demand. We present SearchChord, a training-free framework that leverages a large pool of such candidates, combining text-based preselection, image verification, and latent-field recomposition. Our ablation studies across pool sizes up to 200 candidates show that a pool of 100 offers a cost-effective operating point. Compared with feeding all 100 candidates into recomposition, TextSim preselects a subset of 10 candidates. Rather than uniform averaging, we incorporate image-verifier scores to weight each candidate during recomposition. Experiments on PIE-Bench and the proposed CropWeed-EditBench reveal a trade-off: candidate selection improves target-text alignment, while recomposition better preserves unmodified source content. The oracle verifier achieves the best performance in both preservation and target-text alignment. Practical verification partially recovers semantic alignment, but recomposition still compromises edit fidelity, including in agricultural image edits. These results motivate text-first preselection and stronger verification to balance preservation, fidelity, and cost. Code and data will be made publicly available.

- **Summary**: Pavement crack grid localization remains challenging for thin and tiny cracks due to weak visual cues and background interference. Box detection often misses these cracks, while supervised semantic segmentation faces limited pixel-wise annotations and high labeling costs. - **Summary**: Omnidirectional ($360^\circ$) cameras capture the full surrounding scene in every frame, providing a strong advantage for visual odometry (VO) over pinhole sensors with a limited field of view. However, their equirectangular projection (ERP) introduces latitude-dependent distortion, an artificial seam at the image border, and a sampling bias toward the poles. - **Summary**: Open-vocabulary remote sensing image segmentation (OVRSIS) enables the segmentation of unseen classes without additional annotation or retraining. By analyzing the predictions of existing OVRSIS methods, we find that many misclassified patches coexist with visually similar, correctly classified patches of the same class within the same image. Unfortunately, most of these methods stop after a single inference rather than using their predictions to guide further inference. Motivated by this observation, we propose \textbf{Puzzle}, a plug-and-play iterative re-inference framework that combines prediction history with a visual-similarity prior to progressively refine patch-level attention. - **Summary**: JEPA-based world models predict action consequences in latent space, but representative planning approaches rely on candidate-action search, requiring repeated evaluation of action sequences and incurring substantial online computational cost. We introduce WA-JEPA, a world-action model that couples action generation, action-conditioned latent prediction, and goal-aware feedback. We evaluate short-horizon goal reaching, long-horizon tasks, and full-task execution. The results show that goal-aware predictive feedback supports competitive task performance while maintaining a compact model and low online computational cost. - **Authors**: Jun Seok Kim, Rui Huang
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 5 figures, 4 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Self-supervised monocular depth networks can predict a per-pixel uncertainty, which is mainly evaluated by how well it ranks pixels according to their error. Such metrics are insensitive to the magnitude of the uncertainty, so an uncertainty half as large as the actual error obtains the same score. In this paper, we investigate whether the predicted uncertainty is as large as the depth error, both on average (scale) and across confident and uncertain, near and far pixels (shape). Experiments on KITTI show that MonoCal achieves better calibration across uncertainty levels and depth ranges than rescaling with ground truth, while still using a single image at inference.
