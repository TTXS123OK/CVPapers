### Training-Free Acceleration of Multi-View 3D Transformers via Regime-Aware Attention Approximation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17629v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17629v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-13 00:59:07+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-13 00:59:07+00:00
- **Authors**: Muhammed Izzet Sezer, Sevval Belkis Dikkaya
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multi-view 3D transformers such as VGGT reconstruct a scene from a set of unposed images in a single forward pass, predicting camera poses, depth and 3D points, but the cost of their global attention grows quadratically with the num- ber of images. Accelerators borrowed from large language models assume that each token attends to only a few oth- ers; on these models they lose accuracy or gain little speed. We study the global attention of three multi-view transform- ers on seven datasets and find that every layer plays one of three roles: it averages over the whole scene, matches points across many images, or anchors on the first image, whose coordinates the outputs use. Building on this anal- ysis, we propose Regime-AWare ATTention Approximation (RAWATTA), a training-free method that runs attention in 8- bit precision and keeps 16-bit precision only in the layers where a worst-case calibration check requires it. Its fast set- ting, RAWATTA-Fast, also approximates attention in a few averaging and anchoring layers, while matching layers and the first image keep all their keys. On a 12 GB GPU at 50 im- ages, RAWATTA runs VGGT faster than the released code, mostly through our CUDA engine, and loses at most about one point of pose accuracy (AUC@3) on any dataset; RAWATTA-Fast is slightly faster and less accurate. Both fit 200 images, where the released code runs out of memory at 100.



### Provable Cross-Modal Transfer of Hierarchical Concept Structure in CLIP Embeddings
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17671v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17671v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-13 02:34:52+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-13 02:34:52+00:00
- **Authors**: Nghia Nguyen, Tianjiao Ding, Lachlan Ewen MacDonald, René Vidal
- **Comment**: NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Contrastive vision-language models such as CLIP are trained to align images and text, not to represent taxonomies. Yet empirical studies show that CLIP embeddings exhibit hierarchical structure aligned with the hierarchy of semantic concepts. A theoretical characterization of why such hierarchy emerges from a non-hierarchical objective remains open. In this paper, we formalize this phenomenon as Hierarchical Angular Separation (HAS), a geometric property whereby each non-leaf concept embedding is more similar to its descendants than to off-branch concepts. Empirically, we show that HAS holds approximately in raw images and randomly initialized CLIP image embeddings, while in text embeddings it holds approximately only after contrastive learning. To theoretically explain this asymmetry, we analyze an unconstrained features model in which the image embeddings are fixed and only the text embeddings are trained. At the global minimizer, the text-side HAS violation percentage at each node is at most the image-side HAS violation and near-violation percentage. The key mechanism is the hierarchical co-occurrence structure of image-text pairs, which biases text embeddings toward root-centered image prototypes. For a linear contrastive loss, we derive a closed-form solution that makes this mechanism explicit. We extend the analysis to the InfoNCE loss and establish analogous guarantees under sufficient regularization. Synthetic experiments validate the theory and show that HAS is transferred even beyond the regimes covered by our analysis.

### RaFL: An Empirical Study of Random Filter Losses for Image Reconstruction
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17707v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17707v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-13 03:35:57+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-13 03:35:57+00:00
- **Authors**: Ruibo Wang, Fengbei Liu, Aleksei Tiulpin, Mert R. Sabuncu, Heejong Kim
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Pixel losses train image reconstruction networks toward a point statistic of the target distribution, often averaging away fine textures under uncertainty. Perceptual losses alleviate this issue using pretrained features, but those features are inherently domain-specific. We study a random filter loss (RaFL) that compares prediction and target through filters with random weights, avoiding both pretrained features and task-specific loss engineering. RaFL combines random convolutions, random Fourier features, and random wavelet filters, automatically balancing their contributions through gradient-norm matching, eliminating manual loss-weight tuning and grid search. We systematically investigate three design choices: filter family, weight resampling schedule, and weight initialization. Based on validation PSNR and stability across random seeds, we select the configuration that uses all three filter families, redraws filters every optimization step, and employs Gaussian initialization. We evaluate RaFL on three 2D and two 3D image reconstruction tasks. RaFL outperforms a pixel loss on three tasks and matches it on two, and demonstrates stable performance across random seeds on all five tasks. RaFL adds less than 2% to the backbone's forward FLOPs and no inference cost, requiring $182\times$ fewer loss FLOPs than a VGG19 perceptual loss. These findings suggest that RaFL is a simple, tuning-free, and domain-agnostic loss objective for 2D and 3D image reconstruction.



### QCAP-VQA: Question-Conditioned Capped Prior-Mass Supervision for Medical Visual Question Answering
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17708v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17708v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-13 03:36:34+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-13 03:36:34+00:00
- **Authors**: Yining Zhao, Catarina Moreira, Mukesh Prasad, Ali Braytee
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Medical visual question answering models can answer correctly without attending to the anatomy that the question names. Automatic region priors allow spatial supervision without expert masks, but they are noisy, and it is unclear how strongly they should shape attention and how their effect should be verified. We present QCAP-VQA, which supervises a subset of cross-attention heads with a question-conditioned prior built from ranked segmentation proposals. Its capped prior-mass objective penalizes insufficient attention inside the prior only below a threshold. We evaluate attention against SLAKE structure boxes that play no role in training, separating attention on the named anatomy from how attention shifts between questions about the same image. QCAP-VQA matches the accuracy of a baseline without spatial supervision (84.26% vs. 84.23%), and every evaluated prior-supervised model places more attention on the annotated structures. Over a 64-fold range of regularization strength, the capped objective keeps prior mass between 0.67 and 0.77, whereas a pooled region-mass objective drives it to 0.97 and loses accuracy. The cost lies in question conditioning: as the constraint widens or strengthens, attention shifts agree less with the expert-defined contrast. Across 13 configurations, prior-referenced and expert-referenced evaluation rank the models in largely opposite order (Spearman correlation of -0.87), and the capped models it favors track the prior's own errors. Because clinical use of medical visual question answering requires answers grounded in the queried anatomy, supervision from automatic priors should be judged against independent anatomical references.

- **Summary**: An X-ray angiogram sums every structure along a ray into one pixel. When vessels cross an aneurysm, they obscure part of it and mix their contrast into its filling curve. We present X-Strata, a network that separates a cine angiogram into three layers: the complete aneurysm sac, the vessels in front of it, and the vessels behind it. Recovered 3D sac surfaces have a median Chamfer distance of 0.34 mm on the internal cohort. X-Strata may therefore provide layered aneurysm and vessel information, including 3D sac shape, for patients who undergo only 2D angiography.
