### Virtual Task Prompting for Multi-Task Scene Understanding
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11041v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11041v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-08 00:44:28+00:00

- **Authors**: Zedong Wang, Yucheng Wang, Dan Xu
- **Comment**: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 24 pages, 8 figures, 5 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multi-task scene understanding models typically rely on modified vision encoders or complex decoders for cross-task interaction, which limits their flexibility and compatibility with off-the-shelf vision foundation models (VFMs). We introduce Virtual Task Prompting (VTP), which frames multi-task prompting as retrieving task-adaptive compositions from a shared virtual state, where both the state and routing process are optimized in an end-to-end manner. Concretely, VTP maintains an expanded set of virtual tasks alongside the backbone. At each layer, learnable routing matrices read task-specific compositions as input to interact with image tokens and write the processed results back, forming explicit pathways that capture the intricacies among tasks outside the encoder. Since VTP keeps the backbone clean, it integrates seamlessly with pretrained VFMs, advancing the state of the art on Pascal-Context and NYUD-v2. Stress test on Taskonomy with 13 heterogeneous tasks further shows VTP's robustness in extreme settings. Ablations and analysis indicate that VTP's gains stem from adaptive routing rather than increased capacity. The project page is available at https://jacky1128.github.io/VTP/.



### Differentiable Virtual-Slot Partitioning for Adaptive Tokenization in Vision Transformers
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11058v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11058v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-08 01:21:18+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-08 01:21:18+00:00
- **Authors**: Gaoxiang He, Bin Zhu, Bo Xie, Xiaotong Yu, Yi Chen, Hongfei Yan
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Adaptive tokenization aims to reduce the compute spent on uninformative image regions while preserving accuracy. Existing work relies on (i) fixed or frozen scoring rules, which cannot be trained toward the task loss, or (ii) discrete masks trained with straight-through estimators, which yield noisy gradients, while fully differentiable alternatives instead leave the pre-trained regular grid. We revisit adaptive tokenization from a regular-grid perspective and ask: can a two-scale quadtree be learned end-to-end without deleting a single token during training? We show that one coarse token and its four fine children can be made the two endpoints of a single continuous interpolation. We introduce Differentiable Virtual-Slot Partitioning (\dvsp{}), which keeps four \emph{virtual slots} per $2{\times}2$ region and blends their token features and attention mass with a single weight $α$, exactly reproducing the discrete grid at $α\in \{0,1\}$. On COCO detection with EVA-02-B, \dvsp{} retains $97.9\%$ of dense Box AP while removing $64.2\%$ of visual tokens, giving a $1.61\times$ end-to-end speedup, and it outperforms entropy-based APT, DART, and uniform lower-resolution dense adaptation at matched budgets; these gains depend on the conserved attention mass, whose removal costs $1.59$ Box AP. On a frozen Segmenter it also improves Cityscapes mIoU, and on frozen DeiT backbones it exceeds entropy selection at identical per-image token counts.



### What Does Multiple Choice Actually Measure? Disentangling Video Understanding from Option Exploitation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11062v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11062v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-08 01:30:09+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-08 01:30:09+00:00
- **Authors**: Elliott Piercy, Justin Chan, Sepand Dyanatkar
- **Comment**: Accepted to TAE (Trust-AI-Eval) Workshop at NeurIPS 2026 https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/loc/sydney/178197
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Video understanding evaluations are intended to measure a model's proficiency in video perception and task solving. However, current VideoQA benchmarks struggle to isolate these capabilities due to their reliance on multiple-choice (MC) answer guidance, language priors, and fragile scoring pipelines. Disentangling these confounding factors reveals how much of a model's score is supplied by the evaluation context rather than earned through faithful perception. Across six VideoQA benchmarks of varying length, we find that accuracy is significantly above chance without any visual input. This is caused by shortcutting and option-prior reliance. We express these effects in the language of faithfulness: option-exploitation and answer-conditioned reasoning traces are unfaithful even when they are correct. Together, our results show that a VideoQA score is jointly determined by language priors, option timing, and how outputs and labels are converted into correctness. We produce a novel Option-Delayed Replay (ODR) mechanism that discloses options at controlled stages of an open-ended (OE) trajectory, to obtain objective MC scores from faithful option-free inference. Our work demonstrates that agentic methods with ODR and faithful inference can match option-exploiting performance. Finally, we formalize our diagnostic collection and ODR as a reproducible evaluation protocol, and we release a human-relabelled edition of LVBench (correcting $20.5\%$ of keys) to remove annotation noise as a confounding variable.

### SoftPAT3D: Physics-Augmented Text-to-3D Scene Generation with Deformables
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11087v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.GR, cs.RO
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11087v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-08 02:55:57+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-08 02:55:57+00:00
- **Authors**: Lei Shu, Alberta Longhini, Leonidas Guibas, Gordon Wetzstein, Jeannette Bohg
- **Comment**: 33 pages, 13 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: We present SoftPAT3D, a physics-augmented framework for text-to-3D scene generation in which deformable objects, such as cloth, stuffed toys and ropes, are generated as physically valid, simulation-ready bodies and placed in intersection-free contact with the furniture that supports them, with one another and with themselves. Existing physics-augmented pipelines simulate rigid bodies only and cannot accommodate a generated deformable object, which text-to-3D generation returns as a geometrically invalid mesh, sometimes self-intersecting, that has no rest shape, no material model and no admissible initial state. These are the inputs a deformable simulation requires, and we construct each from information already in the pipeline. The rest shape comes from how the object is manufactured rather than from the generated physically inconsistent deformed configuration, and a material library assigns each deformable object its initial elastic parameters, which can then be optimized. The occluded support is regenerated and converted into a watertight collision mesh, each object is released from an optimizable placement above the supporting part that the prompt specifies, and the complete scene is driven to static equilibrium under one unified contact model, with completion verified by geometric checks and a vision-language model. When a scene fails the prompt, a grid search over placements corrects it, and the adjoint of a differentiable simulation of the same contact energy reaches the targets the grid does not, places several bodies jointly and recovers and optimizes material parameters. Multi-body scenes equilibrate in a single simulation, and the pipeline also runs from the text prompt alone. On all sixty-five prompts, SoftPAT3D produces no undesired interpenetration of any kind, whereas every other system does, and its scenes directly support scene editing and robotic manipulation.



### Does Pose Encoding Matter? Analyzing Geometric Representations for Discrete Motion Vocabulary Learning
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11093v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI, cs.DM
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11093v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-08 03:15:42+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-08 03:15:42+00:00
- **Authors**: Amir Kimiyaie, Nahal Mafi, Hamed Tabkhi
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Compressing continuous human motion into a finite vocabulary of discrete tokens is now the first stage of many motion understanding and generation pipelines. Vector quantization partitions a latent space into Voronoi cells around a finite codebook, but the geometry of that latent space is inherited from the input pose encoding, and it is unclear which encoding admits an efficient discretization. We study four encodings that place skeletal motion in different geometric spaces: Gaussian joint heatmaps on the pixel lattice, which we show to be a morphological dilation of the joint point set by a quadratic structuring function; root-relative joint positions in Euclidean space; local joint rotations on $\SO$ through a continuous 6D embedding; and a redundant full kinematic encoding. For each, we train VQ-VAE tokenizers whose codebooks lie on the unit hypersphere, varying codebook size, temporal stride, and code dimension. Beyond reconstruction, we measure codebook utilization and the effective vocabulary size, i.e., the number of equiprobable spherical Voronoi cells with the same entropy as the observed token distribution. Rotation and position encodings keep converting additional codewords into effective vocabulary, whereas the full kinematic encoding saturates at a few hundred effective cells and heatmaps at fewer than sixty. The smallest code dimension gives the highest utilization for three of the four encodings, and the effect of temporal stride depends on the encoding. Encodings of the same pose family can thus yield effective vocabularies differing by an order of magnitude, a difference that reconstruction loss alone does not reveal, which points to input geometry as a first-order design choice in motion tokenization.

- **Summary**: Visual In-Context Learning (VICL) enables promptable segmentation by conditioning RGB predictions on visual demonstrations, making it attractive for auto-labeling, but its robustness under query-only access and unknown deployment prompts remains underexplored. - **Summary**: Visual in-context segmentation uses visual demonstrations to guide predictions for a new image. In SegGPT, we identify background color mapping deviation, where non-target regions sharing a background label receive different output colors, and part-to-parent expansion, where predictions extend beyond the requested part into its parent object. - **Summary**: Parameter-efficient image-to-video transfer adapts frozen image foundation models to video via lightweight trainable adapters. Existing methods insert the same adapter at every transformer layer, implicitly assuming all layers require equal temporal modeling.
