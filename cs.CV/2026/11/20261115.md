### Cache or Denoise Less? Residual Reuse in a Flow-Matching World Action Model
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19709v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.RO**, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19709v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 01:40:18+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 01:40:18+00:00
- **Authors**: Abhinav Gupta, Nissan Pow
- **Comment**: Honorable Mention at the CoRL 2026 Workshop on Efficient Foundation Models for Real-Time Embodied AI (EEAI). Code and data available at https://github.com/gabhinav001/cache-or-denoise-less
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Flow-matching robot policies generate each action chunk through repeated network evaluations, one per denoising step. Cross-cycle caches such as C$^3$ache reuse computation from earlier replanning calls, but whether they beat simply taking fewer steps is unclear. We compare the two in Fast-WAM, a 6B-parameter world action model, on LIBERO-Goal. Two uncached steps keep the success of ten: on all 500 initial states they are non-inferior within two percentage points, for one checkpoint and noise seed. Under PyTorch's default compile mode, they also cut a replanning call on an RTX A6000 from 202 to 92 ms. This leaves little room for caching. A cache that keeps two fresh steps cannot be faster than two uncached steps: every call still encodes the images, runs the video expert and computes those two steps. Caching the first of two steps, refreshed every fourth call, saves at most 11--14% of a call by our cost model. Cached policies are also fragile. Their losses tracked the share of the flow path the cached steps cover: under Fast-WAM's current time shift, C$^3$ache's eight cached steps cover 80% of the path instead of 44%, and the cached policy succeeds on 63% of states against 95% uncached. Resampling the noise on every call cost caches covering 60% or more of the path 14--40 points. These results argue for fewer-step baselines and for testing reuse under the deployed sampler and noise settings. We release our evaluation harness, logs and a checklist.

- **Authors**: Zhuoran Feng, Xiaoshan Zhou
- **Comment**: 15 pages, 12 figures, 9 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Vision-based monitoring on construction sites fails at night, at dawn and dusk, in tunnels and under floodlights, when conventional RGB cameras under- or over-expose. Event cameras record per-pixel brightness changes with microsecond latency and over 120 dB of dynamic range, but their benefit for site-relevant object detection has not been quantified on real data. We compare three recent event-based detectors (SMamba, SATE and EvRT-DETR) with an RGB detector trained on the same data, using the pixel-aligned PEOD dataset. On low-light frames (70% of the test set) the best event detector reaches 27.2 AP against 21.3 AP for RGB, and a training-free late fusion of both sensors is the best option in every lighting condition (33.3 AP). On rendered construction sites, used zero-shot, dimming the floodlights drops RGB to near zero while the event detector keeps most of its AP50. We close with camera-placement guidance for construction sites.

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19747v1)

- **Comment**: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Spotlight)
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Vision models are sensitive to object-background context, yet it remains unclear how recognition changes as the semantic distance between objects and backgrounds increases. We introduce ImageNet-OOC1k, a benchmark enabling graded control of object-background relationships along a continuous consensus visual-semantic dissimilarity axis. This formulation reveals a highly consistent monotonic decline in recognition accuracy across 35 models spanning convolutional neural networks, Vision Transformers, and vision-language models. - **Authors**: Weihang Yuan
- **Comment**: 8 pages, 2 figures, 4 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Facial expression recognition (FER) degrades sharply when illumination, exposure or resolution departs from the training distribution. Two remedies dominate practice: enriching the input signal, or conditioning intermediate features on an estimated image quality. The two are rarely compared under matched backbones, training budgets and condition annotations, and their cost is seldom reported. We present a controlled comparison on RAF-DB. Input-space conditioning uses four deterministic, label-free views (logarithmic stretching, inverse-logarithmic stretching, down-up resampling and identity), each processed by a shared backbone with a branch-specific BatchNorm and linear head, with the branch logits averaged. Feature-space conditioning is implemented as FiLM and Conditional BatchNorm under an identical protocol. The gain costs 4.00x FLOPs, 4.18x latency and 3.79x training time, i.e. 0.26 percentage points per additional GFLOP, and we state an explicit rule for when the trade is worth making.

- **Summary**: Conventional experience replay typically applies fixed preservation constraints to historical exemplars, overlooking variations in how learned representations, predictions, and anatomical structures change during sequential training. - **Summary**: Auroras provide remote optical observations of energetic electrons precipitating into the upper atmosphere, and whose energy distribution encodes key information about large scale magnetospheric processes. - **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Does an AUROC comparison survive a change in the proportions of tasks, populations, or image corruptions? A pooled-AUROC ranking of failure detectors or uncertainty scores describes one particular mix of groups. We study two fixed scores for a common binary target under mixtures of fixed group-conditional laws. Using the cross-group AUC decomposition, we prove that every rational symmetric quadratic comparison geometry is realizable by finite scalar-score distributions. Universal dominance is thus coNP-hard, and even exhaustive checks of every mixture on fewer than $K$ groups can miss a $K$-group reversal. A grid bound gives a dimension-uniform approximation certificate with sharp $1/m$ order. These results yield exact finite-bank audits, explicit reversal mixtures and distances, and simultaneous population statements. An audit of all 11,019 released Stable LM 2 12B outputs and 35 uncertainty methods finds 66 confirmation-set comparisons that win within all four tasks yet reverse under mixing. Discovery-fitted marginal rank alignment removes all 66; an alignment-error envelope certifies 83 comparisons. Label checks also change several selected reversals. The findings distinguish robustness of frozen scores from robustness after calibration; they do not establish human-error detection, robustness to unseen corruptions, or generalization beyond the evaluated banks.

- **Summary**: In subject-driven text-to-image generation, quantitatively evaluating localized visual consistency across varying poses and environments remains a major challenge. Extensive experiments across four video diffusion models and three tasks show that PoCoAttn consistently achieves a better speed-fidelity Pareto frontier than state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods, improving PSNR by 1.9 to 5.0 dB over baselines and delivering up to 4.1$\times$ attention and 2.0$\times$ denoising speedups over dense FlashAttention-3 attention.

- **Summary**: Vision-based navigation systems for orbit must be trained almost entirely on synthetic imagery, because real labelled images of a given spacecraft in flight rarely exist. Benchmarks like SPEED+ are built for that situation: the training set comes from a simulator, and the test sets are real images of a satellite mockup under two kinds of lighting. That simulator approximates how light reflects off mylar and aluminium, and like every scientific simulator it is misspecified. Its error is radiometric, since it misses the specular highlights, hard shadows and sensor saturation common in real images. We probe the resulting synthetic-to-real gap, the central risk for such systems, with a deliberately simple monocular pose estimator. In a 2x2 study of backbone adaptation and photometric augmentation, trained on synthetic images and tested on both real hardware-in-the-loop domains, we find three things. (1) The model does not know it is failing: regions conformally calibrated to 90% on synthetic data cover only 9-15% of rotation errors on sunlamp and 21-38% on lightbox, so a wrong pose carries the confidence of a correct one. (2) Transfer is asymmetric: scaling data and features improved synthetic rotation error from 1.65 to 1.03 rad but moved the harshly lit sunlamp domain by only 0.05 rad, under twice its seed s.d., whereas adapting the backbone's last stage carried 63-83% of its extra synthetic gain to both real domains. (3) Once the backbone adapts, augmentation helps only in the direction it models: our photometric stack improved the diffusely lit lightbox domain from 1.17 to 0.99 rad on every seed but moved sunlamp no more than seed-to-seed variation, while with the backbone frozen it helped sunlamp more. Every post-review experiment was pre-registered, and the code, per-image predictions and pre-registration record are at https://github.com/lokindoo/spacecraft-pose-estimation.

- **Summary**: Accurate pedestrian network data is foundational to urban accessibility, routing, and infrastructure planning, yet such data remains scarce and difficult to produce at scale.
