### IMPACT: Image-Conditioned Motion with Physics-Aware Collision and Trajectory Guidance for Video Generation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01372v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01372v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-01 00:14:03+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-01 00:14:03+00:00
- **Authors**: Shiwei Yang, Yunlong Yu, Zhao Wang, Jungong Han
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Visually realistic video generation does not guarantee physically consistent motion. Generated objects may exhibit implausible collision responses or rebounds without energy dissipation, while even physically valid motion predictions may not be faithfully realized during synthesis. We propose \textbf{IMPACT}, a physics-state-driven image-to-video framework that addresses these challenges by coupling explicit dynamics prediction with complementary sparse--dense physical guidance. A learnably calibrated Physics State Generator unifies continuous free-flight dynamics with discrete collision responses, linking impact timing, post-impact velocity, rebound attenuation, and eventual settling within a coherent physical-state sequence. To translate this evolution into effective generative constraints, IMPACT derives sparse trajectories and dense region-level motion priors from the same physical rollout. Sparse trajectories constrain global object displacement, while dense priors guide diffusion sampling to encourage coherent object-region motion and suppress unwanted background changes. Their shared physical basis coordinates global and local motion guidance to reduce prediction--generation mismatch. Experiments on 100 controlled sphere--plane collision scenes spanning different material combinations and visual environments demonstrate improved physical plausibility over representative general-purpose, physics-grounded, and motion-controllable baselines. The project repository is available at https://weiweiwiewei.github.io/impact

- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01391v1
- **DOI**: None

- **Authors**: Erfan Mirhaji, Nikhil Krishnaswamy, Jill Zarestky, Lisa Mason, Nathaniel Blanchard, Sarath Sreedharan
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Optimizing vision models for accuracy does not force the model to learn and utilize human-understandable and domain-specific concepts and rules. We propose explaining image classifications through domain-specific concepts, visual evidence, and expert knowledge expressed as logical rules. We introduce FLUENT (Framework for Localized, Uncertainty-Aware Explanations in Normative Terminology), a post-hoc framework that ranks expert-authored rules combining jointly required, alternative, and absent concepts. To account for prediction errors, FLUENT combines predicted labels and concept states with empirically estimated likelihoods and concept priors. It scores each rule by calculating support over satisfying assignments to the concepts mentioned in that rule, then normalizes these scores to express relative confidence among candidate explanations. The selected rule is illustrated through occlusion-based highlights for positive concept mentions in the query image and localized examples in retrieved reference images for negative mentions. These illustrations are intended to show end users unfamiliar with the domain what the concepts named in an explanation look like. FLUENT combines domain knowledge and model accuracy, and delivers visual concept highlights and explicit rule confidence to explain an image in the vocabulary of an application domain.

- **Comment**: None

- **Summary**: Spectral imagery, including hyperspectral image (HSI) and multispectral image (MSI), exhibits substantial heterogeneity in band numbers, wavelengths, spectral resolutions, hindering unified modeling and cross-sensor transfer. From the perspective of imaging physics, these heterogeneous observations can be regarded as discrete measurements of an underlying continuous spectrum acquired through different sampling processes. Motivated by this perspective, we propose SpecUltra, a unified foundation model for heterogeneous spectral images. Specifically, SpecUltra employs 3D convolutional tokenization and wavelength-aware 3D RoPE to encode relative wavelength and spatial relationships, enabling fine-grained spatial-spectral modeling within a shared coordinate system. We further construct MultiHyper-200K, a large-scale multi-sensor HSI dataset, and pretrain SpecUltra using auxiliary-token-assisted mask image modeling (MIM), where a level token indicates the radiometric product type and register tokens absorb sensor- and acquisition-dependent variations. To bridge the spectral-granularity gap between HSI and MSI, we introduce Spectral Kernel Transformation (SKT), which transfers pretrained knowledge in parameter space by constructing MSI embedding kernels from fine-grained HSI-pretrained kernel slices. This design preserves pretrained spectral priors while maintaining compatibility with the pretrained backbone. Experiments on HSI and MSI classification and segmentation benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art or competitive performance across disparate sensors and downstream tasks.

### FOCUS: Closed-Loop Attention Feedback for Efficient Vision-Language Understanding
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01438v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01438v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-01 02:20:07+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-01 02:20:07+00:00
- **Authors**: Akul Santhosh, Aastha Jhunjhunwala
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Processing every video frame through Vision-Language Models (VLMs) adds significant inference cost and limits their ability to comprehend real-time video. Existing approaches either sub-sample frames uniformly, ignoring content and missing important events, or prune visual tokens inside the VLM to cut per-call cost while still invoking the model on every sampled frame. We introduce FOCUS, a training-free three-layer framework that reduces the number of invocations and steers each one towards what matters. Temporal gating measures inter-frame dissimilarity in the patch-embedding space of a smaller VLM from the same family, triggering burst captures only when the scene changes in a semantically meaningful way. Patch-level dissimilarity is then accumulated across the burst and fused with a top-down attention prior into a spatial focus map that encodes where motion and semantic relevance coincide. That map calibrates the VLM's value cache during generation, progressively amplifying vision tokens in task-relevant regions and attenuating off-target ones, without removing any token from the context. The loop closes after each response: text-to-vision cross-attention is extracted from the decoder and fed back as the prior for the next burst, letting the system converge on semantically important regions over successive observations. We evaluate FOCUS on the 500-video EgoSchema subset in streaming mode, with a single Qwen3-VL-8B backbone shared by every method. It reaches 74.8% accuracy using 25.4 VLM calls per clip, against 74.6% with 55 calls for uniform sampling and 74.2% with 55 calls for BOLT. On egocentric video captioning across four domains, it matches the best baseline's quality while issuing 57% fewer VLM invocations than uniform sampling.

- **Summary**: Humans continuously acquire knowledge from carefully crafted materials such as books and tutorial videos, carrying that understanding into future tasks. Together, these results highlight instructional videos as a scalable resource for parameter-update-free agent continual learning.

- **Summary**: Ecological monitoring increasingly relies on camera traps and aerial surveys that generate large image collections. ### See, Think, Use: Diagnosing Perception and Reasoning Failures in VLMs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01467v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01467v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-01 02:56:06+00:00

- **Authors**: Jiacheng Ding, Yanliang Qi, Cong Guo, Peihua Mai
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 4 figures, 8 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: We present See, Think, Use, a protocol that traces each answer of a vision-language model to perception, reasoning or the use of perception. The diagnosis shows a developer which stage to improve and directs test-time compute to the stage that fails. Benchmarks score the final answer, and an answer that rests on the image earns the same credit as a guess or a memorized prior. The protocol asks the model two extra questions per item and needs no access to its weights. A localization probe asks for a box around every instance and measures seeing, and a text-replacement probe answers from a description and measures thinking. Use is the rate at which the answer follows the model's own perception across counterfactual image pairs. The rate needs no answer labels, because the question and its prior are the same on both images. On two model families and six datasets the protocol shows that the knob that helps follows the task family. Answers follow the model's perception except on images that contradict a common prior, and asking the model to localize first repairs this failure when its boxes are right. Allocating compute by the measured stages comes close to the best uniform policy at lower cost and beats a random allocation of the same compute wherever a knob raises the short stage. We will release the code, the prompts and the per-question outputs.

- **Summary**: Visual-text compression renders long contexts as images and is engineered around how much of the text a vision-language model (VLM) can recover. Yet recovered text is not usable text. VLMs can transcribe the text in an image word for word and still fail to answer from it; given back their own transcript, they answer nearly as well as from the original text. Visual-text compression is limited not by how much text a model can recover, but by how much it can use.

- **Summary**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have made substantial progress in 3D scene understanding from multi-view RGB observations. Yet a fixed object and query can elicit inconsistent target-related responses across camera viewpoints, a phenomenon we characterize as viewpoint-induced semantic drift. We introduce LieVLM, a Lie routing framework for view-robust 3D understanding. Motivated by the rotational component of viewpoint change, Lie routing adapts visual representations by applying local $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ transformations to projected features within learned three-dimensional subspaces. Geometry tokens extracted by VGGT predict local rotation parameters and channel-wise routing coefficients. A closed-form Padé--Cayley map transforms projected visual features, whose identity-relative corrections are projected back and gated into the original visual and geometry features. This design retains the pretrained semantic and direct spatial context while learning view-dependent feature adjustments. Experiments on 3D captioning, grounding, detection, and spatial reasoning show consistent improvements over additive fusion across training scales and backbones. Controlled ablations examine the roles of geometric conditioning and transformation structure. Viewpoint analyses further reveal systematic variation in the predicted routing states alongside more localized target responses and stronger task performance across viewpoint changes.
