### Evaluating classification and explainability in multimodal petrographic image analysis
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10331v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10331v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-07 00:02:17+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-07 00:02:17+00:00
- **Authors**: Thomas James Wood, Oktay Karakuş, Can Eyüpoğlu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Automated thin-section classifiers increasingly combine plane-polarised and cross-polarised light (PPL and XPL), two complementary views central to petrographic identification. Classification accuracy alone does not reveal whether model errors reflect mineralogical relationships, how predictions depend on each optical view, or whether attribution maps identify regions that influence model decisions. Here, we examine these complementary aspects of the released LITHOS dual-stream vision transformer using outputs from an earlier evaluation of 105,802 expert-annotated grains across 25 categories. Classification errors frequently followed the label taxonomy: 72.6\% of misclassified plagioclase, microcline and sanidine grains were assigned the generic Feldspar label, while 87.1\% of quartz-family grains received a label within that family, compared with 66.6\% assigned their exact label. Replacing the XPL input with its per-channel spatial means produced a greater reduction in true-class probability than equivalent PPL replacement in 20 of 25 categories, but not in the three quartz categories. Five explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) attribution procedures generated visually distinct maps for identical inputs. In a superpixel-deletion analysis involving 30 grains from 29 thin sections, method-minus-random contrasts ranged from $-0.043$ to $0.057$, with all 41 simultaneous 95\% confidence intervals including zero. Together, these findings show that error structure, modality sensitivity and attribution behaviour capture distinct aspects of model performance that cannot be established from classification accuracy or visual inspection of attribution maps alone.



### TimeTraveler: Temporal Strategy Planning with Time Dictionary for Streaming Video Understanding
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10337v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10337v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-07 00:11:15+00:00

- **Authors**: Minkuk Kim, Junho Kim, Seong Tae Kim
- **Comment**: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Project page: https://geppa.github.io/TimeTraveler
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Streaming video understanding (SVU) requires models to answer user queries over continuously evolving video streams. Unlike offline video QA, SVU must handle queries whose evidence may lie in the past, appear in the current scene, or emerge only after future observations. Existing streaming video systems often rely on compact visual memories or fixed evidence-access schemes, which can lose fine-grained details and fail to adapt the answering process to the temporal intent of each query. In this paper, we propose TimeTraveler, a streaming VQA framework that performs temporal strategy planning with a Time Dictionary. Inspired by the need to search across different temporal regions of a stream, TimeTraveler stores each observed moment as a timestamp-indexed structured caption and uses this dictionary as an explicit source of queryable evidence. Given a query, TimeTraveler determines whether to recall past evidence, read the present context, or wait for future observations, and then applies a strategy-specific evidence acquisition process. Comparative experiments on streaming video QA benchmarks show that TimeTraveler improves streaming question answering by preserving fine-grained temporal evidence and selecting the appropriate temporal strategy for each query. The project page is available at https://geppa.github.io/TimeTraveler.

- **Summary**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) show strong visual perception capabilities, but existing evaluations largely focus on single-view or multi-view settings with shared camera parameters, leaving the challenge of multi-agent collaborative scenarios underexplored. ### PoseBench: Probing Pose Awareness in Visual Foundation Models
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10347v1

- **Authors**: Most Nilufa Yeasmin, Draix Wyatt, Ruyi Lian
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Pose awareness characterizes the extent to which a learned feature space aligns with the underlying object-pose manifold, such that observations with similar poses exhibit greater feature similarity than those with increasingly different poses. While such structure is desirable for pose-sensitive 3D vision tasks, visual foundation models pre-trained on individual 2D images typically lack explicit 3D or pose supervision. This raises a fundamental question: Do 2D visual foundation models exhibit pose awareness after large-scale pretraining? To answer this question, we introduce PoseBench, a systematic benchmark to probe pose awareness in pretrained visual features. Our evaluation follows a progressive design: held-out rendered views probe intrinsic pose-aware structure under controlled conditions, while real-image queries evaluate how robustly this structure remains accessible under real-world variations. We further introduce pose-graph-based refinement as a complementary probe of compatibility between feature neighborhoods and the underlying pose manifold. Across 31 publicly available checkpoints from 10 pretraining families, PoseBench reveals that pose awareness is an implicit but non-uniform property of 2D visual foundation models, and consistent gains from pose-graph refinement suggest that feature spaces retain structure compatible with the underlying pose manifold. PoseBench therefore provides a systematic basis for understanding and selecting pretrained features for pose-sensitive 3D vision tasks.

- **Summary**: Multimodal fusion requires not only exchanging information across modalities but also determining which representations should participate in the interaction and how complementary cross-modal views should be combined. - **Summary**: Domain adaptive point cloud completion (DA PCC) aims to bridge the geometric discrepancy between labeled source domains and unlabeled target domains for accurate 3D shape completion. - **Summary**: Transparent-interface reconstruction must recover glass surfaces even when most observed color comes from the scene behind them.
