- **Authors**: Alexandr A. Kalinin, Dihan Zheng, Taylla Milena Theodoro, Ivan Ivanov, Eduardo Hirata-Miyasaki, See-Chi Lee, Aofei Liu, Sricharan Reddy Varra, Talon Chandler, Soorya Pradeep, Chad Liu, Manuel D. Leonetti, Carolina Arias, Bo Huang, Shalin B. Mehta
- **Comment**: 64 pages: 10-page main text with 4 figures and 1 table, plus references and a 49-page appendix with 15 figures and 25 tables. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026, Track on Evaluations and Datasets
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Understanding how cells respond to perturbations over time is central to cell biology and drug discovery. Live-cell imaging captures the dynamics of perturbation-induced phenotypes in individual cells. While fluorescence imaging can visualize specific molecular phenotypes, labeling and repeated illumination often affect cell health. Virtual staining offers an alternative by predicting these fluorescence images from images acquired without fluorescent labels. Although many virtual staining models have been developed, their performance on volumetric time-lapse data remains poorly characterized. We address this gap with an evaluation suite for 3D time-lapse virtual staining of live cells. The suite combines paired 3D time-lapse images acquired with and without fluorescent labels, a set of baseline models, and three metric tiers measuring pixel-level fidelity, organelle segmentation performance, and single-cell phenotype similarity. Using this evaluation suite, we study metric and model behavior across 3 cell types, 2 microscopes, 4 organelles, and 3 perturbation states. We find that regression baselines better predict the spatial localization of target organelles, whereas the generative baseline better captures the distribution of phenotypes across cells. These differences expose trade-offs between structural and phenotypic fidelity that are obscured by single-metric evaluation and suggest how virtual staining models can be developed for diverse downstream tasks such as localization, segmentation, tracking, and phenotypic profiling. We release the curated datasets, baseline checkpoints, and evaluation code as part of DynaCell, a broader framework for image-based profiling of cell-state dynamics.

- **Summary**: Computed tomography (CT) provides diverse patient information and various clinical tasks, for which numerous deep learning models have been investigated. - **Summary**: Recent interactive video world models generate visually compelling videos from a single image, following user-provided camera motion and text prompts. - **Comment**: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026. 12 figures, 12 tables. Code: https://github.com/VAIL-Fan/TeSCo
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Cross-view object correspondence (CVOC) aims to establish associations of the same target between query and search videos captured from different viewpoints. Existing methods often rely on visual cues for CVOC. Despite recent progress, these methods struggle in complicated scenarios with substantial viewpoint changes and appearance variations, due to lacking sufficient target information. Addressing this, we introduce TeSCo, a framework that exploits rich Textual Semantics of an object, in addition to its visual cues, for cross-view object Correspondence. Our key insight is, textual description of a target, by capturing diverse attributes such as category, texture, and color, provides viewpoint- and appearance-invariant semantics, which are complementary to visual cues and can hence enhance correspondence robustness in complicated scenes. Inspired by this, TeSCo first generates a textual expression of the target from the given query view using a vision-language model, and then extracts textual feature to enhance visual features for localization in the search view. To realize this, we introduce a text-conditioned cross-modal fusion (TCF) module that incorporates the textual feature into visual features with text-guided modulation, producing more robust multimodal target representation for object correspondence. Since not all textual cues are equally compatible with the target in current search-view frame, we propose an iterative textual feature refinement (ITFR) module that progressively adjusts textual feature using search-view information before the TCF module, enabling search-view-aware textual semantics to be fused into visual features and leading to better performance. In extensive experiments on Ego-Exo4D and HANDAL-X, TeSCo demonstrates state-of-the-art results and largely outperforms other models, validating its efficacy. Our code is released at https://github.com/VAIL-Fan/TeSCo.

### Do Visual RL Gains Scale at Test Time? A Quantile View of Post-Training
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02366v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02366v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-02 01:26:57+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-02 01:26:57+00:00
- **Authors**: Austin Wang, Lexing Ying
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training improves the single-sample quality of visual generative models, but deployment often provides additional inference compute through Best-of-N sampling. Whether RL gains transfer under such test-time scaling, particularly on difficult held-out tasks, remains poorly understood in the visual domain. Our central contribution is to systematically identify and characterize a failure mode of scaling transfer in visual RL: across multiple RL methods, model backbones, and evaluations spanning compositional generation, text rendering, and preference alignment, higher single-sample reward does not necessarily imply a better quality-compute frontier. RL gains may attenuate, saturate, or even reverse at large sampling budgets, with the base model eventually outperforming its post-trained counterpart. We explain these regimes through a quantile-transfer framework, showing that Best-of-N places increasing weight on the upper tail of the reward distribution as N grows. Consequently, RL can improve typical samples while compressing or degrading the rare high-quality outcomes that test-time scaling exploits. As a simple intervention motivated by this analysis, we introduce Quantile Utility Policy Optimization (QUPO), which optimizes a controllable mixture of quantile utilities corresponding to different inference budgets. Experiments on SD3.5-M and BAGEL show that QUPO better preserves upper-tail quality and substantially improves scaling on challenging held-out prompts relative to standard RL post-training. Our results suggest that visual generative models should be evaluated and optimized not only for single-sample quality, but for how post-training reshapes the full reward distribution that inference-time scaling exploits.
