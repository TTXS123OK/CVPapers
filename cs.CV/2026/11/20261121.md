- **Summary**: Few-shot object detection in agricultural imagery is hampered by dense vegetation, repeated patterns, and scarce annotations. We present a training-free detector that turns dense features from frozen visual foundation models directly into detections, without fine-tuning, a learned head, or external proposals: a whitened foreground-background direction estimated from annotated support images scores every query patch, and the score field is decoded with an adaptive threshold and support-derived geometry. ### CAMA: Calibrated Anti-Collapse Marginal Alignment for Test-Time Adaptation of Vision-Language Models
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28461v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28461v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 01:29:20+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 01:29:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Jinho Kang, Yunhui Guo
- **Comment**: 30 pages, 11 figures, 18 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Test-time adaptation (TTA) updates a model using unlabeled test data to help it handle distribution shifts. However, prior work has found that image corruption causes a vision-language model (VLM) to produce more similar features for different images, a phenomenon known as feature compression. Under compression, entropy minimization, the most common TTA objective, can cause the model to favor a few classes and perform worse than before adaptation. Despite these observations, a detailed analysis of how these failures are connected and an effective strategy for preventing them are still lacking.   In this paper, we first analyze why entropy minimization fails for VLMs. We then propose CAMA, a new test-time adaptation objective that maximizes batch mutual information to encourage confident predictions for individual images while maintaining diverse predictions across the batch. CAMA also estimates a reference class distribution early in the test stream and aligns each batch's average prediction with this reference during adaptation. We prove that feature compression biases the predictions and that entropy minimization amplifies this bias into collapse, whereas CAMA is guaranteed to preserve every class. Across standard corruption benchmarks, we observe collapse on nearly every VLM backbone evaluated. CAMA prevents collapse in every observed case and, in turn, achieves the best accuracy on every backbone. Quantitative and qualitative analyses confirm that CAMA reverses the feature compression behind the collapse, and ablations show that each of its components contributes.

- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: The rapid advancement of AI-generated content (AIGC) video generation and editing technologies has raised growing societal concerns about the authenticity and reliability of digital videos. Existing video detection methods primarily exploit temporal inconsistencies through holistic frame-level representations, whereas manipulation localization methods focus on artifacts within individual spatial regions. However, holistic representations may obscure localized artifacts through global feature aggregation, while region-centric approaches may overlook disagreements between individual regions and their surrounding spatiotemporal context. To address these limitations, we propose VIdeo Contextual Agreement (VICA), a unified framework that masks individual video regions and leverages a frozen V-JEPA model to predict their representations from the surrounding spatiotemporal context. By comparing predicted and observed representations, VICA derives spatial residuals that characterize intra-frame discrepancies and bidirectional temporal residuals that capture inconsistencies across preceding and subsequent frames. These residuals characterize contextual disagreements, providing a shared forensic representation for both tasks and shifting the focus from identifying isolated manipulation artifacts to assessing regional agreement with expectations inferred from the broader video context. Extensive experiments demonstrate that VICA achieves state-of-the-art performance on both tasks across multiple benchmarks, with strong generalization to unseen video generators and editing methods.

- **Summary**: Vision Banana showed that a closed image generator, instruction-tuned to paint perception outputs as RGB images, becomes a generalist vision model. - **Summary**: Understanding naturalistic brain activity requires going beyond the stimulus alone, as external inputs continuously interact with an individual's evolving brain state. - **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reasoning-based driving vision-language-action (VLA) models propose multiple candidate trajectories at each decision, yet only one is executed. Standard evaluation scores the best candidate, assuming an oracle selector that deployment lacks, and leaves the risk of the executed candidate unexamined. Existing online evaluators typically close this gap with a purpose-trained scene or world model, often rolling out each candidate through it at inference time. We introduce AlpaSafe, a risk-aware trajectory selector that reuses the scene representation a frozen driving VLA computes while proposing its candidates. AlpaSafe reads the last hidden token of the VLA's prompt prefill. A fixed rule removes candidates dominated on the two risk logits and selects the highest-scoring candidate among those remaining, adding neither rollouts nor VLA training. Experiments on 525 AlpaSim scenes show that AlpaSafe outperforms Alpamayo's default first-sample rule and uniform random selection in selected regret and top-1 agreement, and reduces the collision-at-fault or offroad rate from 6.41% and 6.10% to 4.93%. The selector has 1.7M parameters and takes 0.75 ms per decision.

- **Summary**: Jev-style system-1 decision models read a choice from a single prefill. They restrict the next-token distribution to the supplied option labels and return a distribution over the options, with no generation and no parsing. Recent work shows that generative vision-language models are sensitive to the relative order of image and text tokens. We ask whether that sensitivity reaches this Jev-style readout.
