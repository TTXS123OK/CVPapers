### Split-and-scale Latent 3D Representations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16126v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16126v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-12 00:23:33+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-12 00:23:33+00:00
- **Authors**: Dorian Chan, Muhammed Kocabas, Xiaoming Zhao, Oncel Tuzel, Jen-Hao Rick Chang
- **Comment**: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Latent 3D representations have significantly expanded the capabilities of modern shape modeling, enabling compact encoding of 3D scenes and supporting powerful 3D generative models. Despite this progress, current representations struggle to capture the fine details of complex objects, fundamentally limited by the capacity of their latents. However, scaling latent capacity at training time is computationally expensive and often infeasible under memory limits, and naively increasing the number of latents at inference fails to make effective use of the added capacity. In this paper, we address both challenges with \textit{split-and-scale}, a method that effectively expands latent capacity at inference while keeping training cost tractable. Our key observation is that by representing 3D scenes as continuous functions of 3D coordinates, a single pretrained tokenizer can be applied at inference to arbitrarily small sub-regions of a scene, allocating the same latent capacity to a smaller spatial extent and thus capturing far higher detail. We validate this insight by conducting extensive ablations to study the trade-offs between compute, memory, latent capacity, and reconstruction quality, demonstrating our inference-time scaling method achieves quality competitive with much larger models that exceed single-GPU memory limits. We further demonstrate that split-and-scale supports high-quality generative modeling, training an autoregressive 3D generator that performs competitively with state-of-the-art baselines.

- **Summary**: Cross-view pose estimation estimates a ground camera's geographic pose by matching onboard sensor data with geo-referenced overhead imagery. This is particularly relevant when GNSS is unreliable, for example under forest canopy or in urban canyons. Existing metric cross-view benchmarks, such as VIGOR and cross-view KITTI, are dominated by urban and road scenes with strong human-made structure without off-road terrain. We introduce GOOSELoc, a multimodal benchmark for ground-to-aerial localization in unstructured environments, including forests, fields, and rural tracks. ### SPEM: Scan-Prior Entropy Modeling for LiDAR Point Cloud Compression
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16212v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16212v1)

- **Authors**: Bojun Liu, Yangzhi Ma, Jie Li, Dong Liu, Li Li
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LiDAR point clouds exhibit scan-induced spatial regularities that are informative for geometry compression. Existing scan-aware approaches often exploit these regularities by transforming coded geometry into spherical or beam-aligned coordinate systems. However, this couples acquisition priors with geometry representation, altering spatial partitioning and quantization, and potentially preventing exact recovery of the original Cartesian geometry. We argue that acquisition priors should guide probability modeling rather than dictate the coding representation. We propose SPEM, a Scan-Prior Entropy Modeling framework that exploits LiDAR scanning regularities within the entropy model without requiring coordinate transformation. We introduce a multi-frequency spherical rotary positional encoding that captures scan-aligned spatial relationships in both backbone self-attention and decoded-context retrieval. Building upon this encoding, we develop a decoded-memory autoregressive predictor that efficiently exploits previously decoded occupancy information through reusable key/value caches. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in both lossy and lossless LiDAR geometry compression, highlighting the effectiveness of scan-prior modeling across different coordinate representations.



### WorldDraft: If Code Builds the World, Can Video Models Just Render It?
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16217v1
- **DOI**: None

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16217v1)

- **Authors**: Zhiyu Hou, Wen-Jie Shu, Harold Haodong Chen, Weilin Ruan, Chenfei Liao, Liuzhou Zhang, Disen Lan, Xiaogang Xu, Qi Chen, Ser-Nam Lim
- **Comment**: Project: https://haroldchen19.github.io/WorldDraft-site/
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal coding agents decouple 4D world construction from visual generation, repurposing video models from world generators toward generative renderers. This division of labor raises a fundamental question: if code builds the world, can video models just render it? A reliable generative renderer hinges on two complementary criteria: (i) world preservation, retaining prescribed 4D kinematics and camera paths; and (ii) visual realization, persistently binding text-specified target attributes over time. To evaluate this capability, we introduce WorldDraft, a benchmark of $432$ strictly paired test cases across $48$ executable 4D world families spanning six dynamic challenge categories. Under a $3 \times 3$ factorial design, WorldDraft orthogonally crosses draft granularity (abstract proxy to previs) and appearance alignment (neutral to conflicting) under identical dynamics. Extensive evaluations across $12$ representative models reveal that: (I) world preservation and visual realization remain in stark operational tension; (II) richer draft scaffolding anchors structure but paradoxically impairs appearance fidelity; and (III) conflicting source cues trigger severe source leakage (up to $64\%$), exposing fragile cross-modal disentanglement. To mitigate these bottlenecks, we propose WorldDraft-Primer, a plug-and-play visual prompting strategy that consistently improves both objectives across frozen video renderers. Together, WorldDraft and WorldDraft-Primer lay a foundation for bridging programmatic 4D simulation with video generation, advancing physically grounded generative rendering.



### Disentangled Embedding Optimization for Diffusion-Based Dataset Distillation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16224v1
- **DOI**: 10.1145/3767308.3835115
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16224v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-12 03:18:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-12 03:18:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Hongcheng Li, Yucan Zhou, Yibin Wang, Xiaoyan Gu, Bo Li, Weiping Wang
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 7 figures. Accepted at the 34th ACM International Conference on Multimedia (ACM MM 2026)
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Dataset distillation aims to compress large-scale datasets into compact synthetic subsets that enable efficient model training with comparable performance to the original data. Recent advances show that diffusion models are highly effective for this task due to their strong generative capability. Existing diffusion-based approaches typically perform class-wise clustering and use the obtained cluster prototypes as diffusion inputs to generate distilled samples. However, the uniformly distributed original samples in a category inevitably lead to entanglement near cluster boundaries, pulling the cluster prototypes closer and reducing the inter-cluster separation. Consequently, the diffusion model receives overly similar latent inputs, resulting in synthetic data with limited diversity. To address this issue, we propose Disentangled Embedding Optimization (DEO), a simple yet effective framework that replaces static clustering with trainable embeddings. These embeddings are optimized to match the Gaussian statistics (i.e., mean and covariance) of the original data distribution, while a diversity regularizer is designed to encourage separation among these embeddings to improve the intra-class variability. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our proposed DEO can generate more diversified and representative distilled data than existing cluster-based diffusion methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance and superior generalization across multiple datasets.



### ATune: Unsupervised Photo Retouching Through Image-Editor Controls
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16236v1
- **DOI**: None

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16236v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-12 03:36:20+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-12 03:36:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Divyanshu Goyal, Gabriel Ruiz, Amir Meisami
- **Comment**: 27 pages, 12 figures, 18 tables. Main paper 8 pages plus supplementary material
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Photo retouching models are trained on pairs of original and retouched photographs, usually edited by experts. Expert edits are costly, so the two main public sets, FiveK and PPR10K, hold only 16,161 originals. Good photographs on their own are far easier to find: commercial stock collections and free ones such as Pixabay and Unsplash hold millions. We learn retouching from them alone: we damage each photograph the way a camera or a careless edit would, which gives a worse and a better version of the same photograph. From these pairs, our model, ATune, learns how 13 measured attributes, such as brightness, contrast and sharpness, should change to raise a photograph's quality. The edit is declarative: ATune sets target values for the attributes, and a simple search adjusts the sliders of an out-of-the-box photo editor until the photograph reaches them. Every edit is a list of slider values a person can read and adjust. The same model can drive any editor that makes those changes; we test RawTherapee and darktable. We train on 10M photographs from a commercial stock collection and test on FiveK, PPR10K and SPAQ. Nine quality judges score every edit, and in a user study 157 human raters made 9,253 choices between our edit and another method's edit of the same photograph. When Claude Opus 5 and GPT-6 Astra edit the same photographs in the same editor, raters prefer our edits to GPT-6 Astra's (54.3%) and rate them even with Claude's (50.5%). We also compare with supervised retouchers on their home ground: NeurOP on FiveK and AdaInt on PPR10K, each trained on that set's own expert edits. Raters chose our edits in 46.3% of those comparisons. ATune, a 54M-parameter model trained without a single expert edit, now matches frontier models at setting an editor's sliders.

- **Summary**: Dynamic reconstruction from casual video must preserve valid subject motion while suppressing transient occluders, shadows, and volumetric effects. - **Summary**: Distinguishing different heat sources in low-resolution infrared (IR) images is inherently difficult, as multiple thermal emitters often collapse into ambiguous and overlapping responses. - **Summary**: AI-generated image detectors face significant challenges when deployed in real-world environments, particularly when test samples are produced by unseen generators that deviate from the source training distribution. ### Selective Generative Restoration for Video Deraining via Uncertainty-Aware Diffusion

- **Summary**: Video deraining is challenging due to rain streaks, occlusions, and atmospheric effects. Existing methods mainly follow a deterministic restoration paradigm, assuming that clean scenes can be uniquely recovered from rainy observations. However, rain degradation is spatially heterogeneous. Reliable regions require faithful reconstruction, whereas severely occluded regions contain ambiguous content that require stronger prior-driven inference. Therefore, conventional deterministic restoration often produces over-smoothed results, while global generative restoration may introduce unnecessary hallucinations. We introduce a Selective Generative Restoration paradigm for video deraining, where generative priors are adaptively activated according to restoration uncertainty. Furthermore, an anchor-guided trajectory consistency mechanism is proposed to regularize stochastic diffusion evolution across frames, reducing temporal generation drift and improving video coherence. To achieve practical efficiency, SGVD adopts one-step diffusion inference while maintaining uncertainty-aware generative capability. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SGVD achieves superior restoration performance, perceptual quality, and temporal consistency compared with state-of-the-art video deraining methods.

- **Summary**: Calibrated multi-view hand reconstruction has advanced rapidly, yet the effectiveness of existing methods depends strongly on real captured data, which is costly to acquire, limited in hand-geometry diversity, and often affected by imperfect annotations. - **Summary**: Generative image editing facilitates content creation but complicates source attribution and edit verification.
