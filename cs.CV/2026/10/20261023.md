### MAGIC: Margin-gated Contrastive Debiasing with Evidence Restoration for VLM Test-time Robustness Scaling
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32013v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32013v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-23 00:24:44+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-23 00:24:44+00:00
- **Authors**: Shunshun Gu, Enze Zhang, Ming Pei, Hang Zhou, Xiao Luo
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: This paper studies the problem of test-time robustness scaling for large vision-language models (LVLMs), which aims to mitigate language bias during inference. Existing approaches typically adopt test-time scaling techniques such as contrastive decoding to improve inference performance. Despite the progress, the performance of these approaches is far from satisfactory due to intentional evidence removal during perturbations and redundant inference cost. Towards this end, we propose a novel approach named Margin-gated Contrastive Debiasing with Evidence Restoration (MAGIC). The core of our MAGIC is to evaluate the decision margins of LVLMs to selectively activate textual and visual perturbation branches for efficient debiased inference. In particular, our MAGIC first measures the prediction margins of crucial tokens, and applies a gating function to identify low-confidence responses that are susceptible to language biases. For selected samples, we integrate the token logits from multiple textual and image perturbation branches with the original ones for debiased decision. More importantly, we selectively restore the most informative corrupted views with sharp prediction distributions for ambiguous samples after debiasing, enabling evidence recovery for discriminative decision-making. We further utilize a calibration dataset to actively identify and prune ineffective branches during contrastive debiasing for cost reduction. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets validate the effectiveness of the proposed MAGIC in comparison with extensive baselines.

- **Authors**: Joonhyeok Jeong, Jy-yong Sohn, Kibok Lee
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Supervised contrastive learning and its balanced variants have demonstrated their effectiveness for representation learning under long-tailed distributions. However, although prior analyses suggest that their optimal representations may exhibit a regular simplex geometry, whether such a geometry is indeed exhibited remains insufficiently understood. In this work, we present a theoretical framework for these objectives that characterizes whether a regular simplex is formed at the optimum. By applying this to representative objectives, we show that these objectives fail to form a regular simplex at the optimum because they remain dependent on the number of samples per class. Motivated by this analysis, we introduce structural balancing that can be applied to contrastive objectives, and propose a fully balanced contrastive objective that provably forms a regular simplex geometry at its optimum. Both theoretical and empirical results demonstrate that the proposed method yields a more balanced representation space and improves performance on long-tailed recognition and transfer learning tasks.

- **Comment**: None

- **Summary**: Cross-lingual dubbing aims to convert source speech into target-language speech that remains synchronized with the original video. The key to this task is to produce a faithful translation that is naturally speakable within the source timing, which is non-trivial because languages differ in duration, rhythm, and articulation. In this paper, we propose X-Dubbing, which formulates the task as a joint problem of time-aware speech-to-text translation and video-aware text-to-speech. Extensive experiments show that X-Dubbing outperforms recent baselines across diverse metrics, including isochrony and audio-visual correspondence.

- **Summary**: Diffusion-based image generation models produce high-quality images but may generate harmful or policy-violating content, motivating machine unlearning (MU) to selectively remove target concepts while preserving general capabilities. ### RADIANT: Object-Centric Feed-Forward Global Illumination

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32075v1)

- **Authors**: Sarah Zhang, Zhiyang Dou, Minghao Guo, Benjamin Tod Jones, Wojciech Matusik
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Feed-forward neural renderers synthesize global illumination across novel 3D scenes without per-scene optimization or runtime ray tracing. Existing transformer-based renderers tokenize individual triangles, so self-attention scales quadratically with mesh resolution. Yet macroscopic radiative transport is governed by the underlying surface geometry and object arrangements rather than a particular mesh discretization; finer tessellation can thus inflate token counts without changing the underlying transport physics, motivating a discretization-agnostic and object-centric representation. We introduce RADIANT (RADiance-based Inter-Actor Neural Transformer), an object-centric architecture for feed-forward global illumination from point inputs. By representing each object with a compact budget of 32 surface-patch tokens, RADIANT decouples attention sequence length from polygon counts. In a single network, an inter-object scene transformer models 3D radiative exchange and a view decoder renders images at target camera poses. For primary visibility without mesh connectivity, camera rays cross-attend scene tokens guided by depth-tested point splats in a shared spherical frame. Across a 1.03M-scene benchmark under matched model capacity, RADIANT reaches 25 dB tone-mapped PSNR in 5x fewer training steps than RenderFormer, and achieves 32.2 dB at 600k steps (+3.3 dB over RenderFormer and +7.6 dB over RenderFormer-V2). We further demonstrate the scalability of RADIANT, which completes a 512x512 forward pass of a 500-object scene in 0.83 s, with computational cost scaling with the number of objects rather than polygons.

- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Proactive streaming video understanding requires assistants to track unfolding events and respond when a user's requested condition is met. To make these decisions, assistants must accumulate relevant evidence and reassess response conditions as new observations arrive. Existing work offers complementary capabilities for this process: proactive video assistants learn when to respond, while streaming video reasoning methods generate scheduled intermediate text to organize observations and support subsequent answers. Together, these capabilities provide a foundation for proactive reasoning, but how to coordinate ongoing reasoning with timely responses remains underexplored. We construct 189K dual-stream examples and train SyncPro through supervised fine-tuning followed by trajectory-level reinforcement learning. Experiments across three proactive benchmarks show improved response timing and more accurate answers to user requests.
