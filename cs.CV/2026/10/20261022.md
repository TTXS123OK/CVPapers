### Visual Grounding First, Multimodal In-context Learning Follows
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30592v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30592v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-22 01:48:28+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-22 01:48:28+00:00
- **Authors**: Minhyuk Seo, Minjae Lee, Chaeeun Lee, Wei Lin, Muhammad Jehanzeb Mirza, Tinne Tuytelaars, Jonghyun Choi
- **Comment**: NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LLMs have shown strong in-context learning (ICL) capability, but extending it to Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) remains challenging. Prior multimodal ICL methods often rely on ICL-specific datasets for additional training or task vector extraction, which can improve performance on the same ICL benchmark used for adaptation. However, they do not generalize well to other benchmarks and often induce forgetting in previously well-solved tasks. For this reason, we identify 'visual grounding' as the key bottleneck in multimodal ICL; MLLMs often fail to attend to task-relevant visual evidence in demonstrations, instead over-relying on textual cues and producing hallucinated outputs. To address this, we propose ProCoRe to enhance visual grounding through contrastive reinforcement learning on multimodal contrastive data for improving multimodal ICL. ProCoRe generates captions for contrastive image pairs, decomposes them into propositions, and optimizes contrastive rewards that encourage alignment with corresponding images while discouraging mismatched ones. Despite never seeing ICL-formatted training examples, ProCoRe improves average multimodal ICL classification accuracy by 5.6% and captioning ROUGE-L by 13.8% over the off-the-shelf Qwen-VL-3-8B model, outperforming ICL-data-dependent state of the arts across benchmarks. Finally, we introduce PICL, a personalized multimodal ICL captioning benchmark that tests whether MLLMs genuinely understand visual demonstrations rather than relying on pre-trained semantic priors.



### KinoUAV: A Benchmark for Safe and Stable UAV Vision-Language Navigation with Kinodynamic Modeling
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30609v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30609v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-22 02:16:48+00:00
- **Authors**: Chenyao Yang, Peijin Wang, Yingchao Feng, Wenhui Diao
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have recently emerged as important remote sensing platforms, and UAV navigation has shown great potential in remote sensing applications. However, existing methods do not explicitly incorporate kinodynamic modeling or safety distances into trajectory generation, resulting in high absolute yaw rates, frequent collisions, and low navigation success rates, which limit safety and motion stability. To address this challenge, we introduce KinoUAV, a kinodynamic benchmark comprising a dataset and KinoUAV-Agent. First, we develop a hierarchical kinodynamic trajectory generation pipeline that drives UAV through acceleration actions. It uses waypoint chains from global planning and generates trajectories segment by segment under velocity, acceleration, and jerk constraints, reducing oscillations and improving trajectory stability. Furthermore, we introduce directional free-space distance (DFD) labels into the constructed dataset to describe the available clearance for safe motion decisions. The proposed KinoUAV-Agent predicts DFDs and uses them to generate safe actions, decoupling perception from action decision-making. This design enables autonomous obstacle avoidance and improves navigation safety. The benchmark incorporates the proposed Cruise-Seek task, in which the UAV first enters the target region and then searches for the target, reducing the difficulty of target search and improving navigation success. Experiments on KinoUAV show that KinoUAV-Agent outperforms existing methods, achieving the highest success rate while maintaining lower absolute yaw rates and collision rates. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of kinodynamic modeling for safe and stable UAV navigation.

- **Summary**: Unsupervised federated learning with Vision-Language Models (e.g., CLIP) offers a promising solution for label-free classification, yet its performance often degrades under severe non-IID shifts. A pioneering method, FedCoPL, typically relies on self-training strategies that exclusively prioritize high-confidence predictions for pseudo-labeling. However, this selective approach neglects the information of ambiguous samples (often referred to as ``waist" samples), leading to the underutilization of data and biased decision boundaries. - **Summary**: Self-supervised Vision Transformers learn strong image-level representations, but their patch-level representations can be unreliable for dense prediction. ### Asking Less, Adapting Better: Feedback-Efficient Test-Time Adaptation for Vision-and-Language Navigation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30643v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30643v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-22 03:05:25+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-22 03:05:25+00:00
- **Authors**: Zixu Wang, Huaquan Zhang, Shoukai Xu, Jinwu Hu, Zhiquan Wen, Mingkui Tan
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) requires agents to follow instructions and navigate to target locations. However, current VLN agents often suffer severe performance degradation in unseen environments due to distribution shifts. Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) offers a promising paradigm to bridge this gap, yet existing methods that rely on unsupervised entropy minimization can propagate errors through the trajectory, causing cascading failures in navigation. Recent methods mitigate this by incorporating human feedback, but they apply trajectory-level supervision uniformly across all steps without localizing the actions is truly informative, leading to inefficient feedback utilization and mismatched optimization granularity. To address these limitations, we propose FETA, a Feedback-Efficient Test-Time Adaptation method that improves both feedback efficiency and optimization reliability. To reduce unnecessary human interventions, we introduce a hierarchical sample selection strategy to localize informative samples. Specifically, we first filter trajectories whose normalized policy entropy exhibits large fluctuations. Within each selected trajectory, we further select the critical action via an entropy-gradient saliency criterion and query a single binary feedback on its correctness. A discrepancy-aware entropy optimization objective then updates the policy only at the queried step, with adaptive scaling that assigns larger relative weights to samples with greater prediction-feedback discrepancy. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that FETA achieves state-of-the-art performance with fewer human interventions, while remaining robust under noisy feedback and continual adaptation settings.

- **Summary**: Federated unsupervised domain adaptation (FUDA) addresses the challenge of adapting models to an unlabeled target domain using decentralized source domains while preserving data privacy.
