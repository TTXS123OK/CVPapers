- **Comment**: Project page: https://haoyu-zhao.github.io/CLE/index.html

- **Summary**: Continual learning is the ability of LLM agents to improve over time by acquiring, refining, and applying knowledge from experience while retaining useful prior knowledge. It is essential for agents learning over long time horizons, yet current evaluations largely assess individual task performance rather than improvement through accumulated experience. We introduce Continual Learning Exam (CLE), a large-scale benchmark developed by 50+ domain experts to measure experience-driven improvement in realistic professional workflows. CLE comprises 120 human-authored tasks, each organised as a five-level ladder, yielding 600 levels spanning 89 professional roles across 12 occupational domains. Agents must pass each level to unlock the next, with later levels building on experience accumulated during earlier assignments. An evaluation agent assesses each submission against human-authored rubrics. We compare a single-submission setting with one that provides diagnostic feedback after failure and allows one revision. Across 17 harness-model configurations using frontier LLMs, feedback and revision yield a median completion gain of 5.3 percentage points. Yet the best-performing system clears only 47.7% of levels and completes all five levels in just 23.3% of tasks, highlighting the difficulty of sustained learning from experience for existing frontier LLMs. As such, the dataset can also be used to post-train LLMs to strengthen their continual learning capabilities.

- **Summary**: Automated classification of gastrointestinal (GI) findings in endoscopic images could support early diagnosis, but architectures are often chosen on accuracy alone. We present a controlled, reproducible benchmark of ResNet50 and ViT-B/16 for eight-class GI classification on the public Kvasir dataset (4,000 images), using a stratified split (400 test images) and stratified group 5-fold cross-validation over all images. In cross-validation, ResNet50 and ViT-B/16 reached 92.12% and 91.70% accuracy, and a two one-sided tests (TOST) procedure showed equivalence within a +/-3 percentage-point margin (difference +0.43 points, 90% CI -0.33 to +1.18; p < 0.001). Accuracy differences between architectures were thus small relative to the effects of shortcut learning and domain shift, whereas efficiency differences were large. An efficient CNN is a defensible default for resource-constrained screening, but no model is deployment-ready without local validation.

- **Summary**: Deep learning facilitates rapid single shot three dimensional (3D) reconstruction using fringe projection profilometry (FPP), but recovering accurate surface height from a single high frequency fringe image remains challenging because both long-range spatial consistency and fine geometric details must be preserved. Convolutional networks are limited in modeling long range dependencies, whereas window-based Transformers establish global interactions only progressively with increasing depth. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of combining selective state space modeling with hierarchical window based attention for accurate single-shot FPP reconstruction.

- **Summary**: Camera motion recognition requires separating viewpoint changes caused by camera translation, rotation, or zoom from object motion and scene dynamics. Video-language models often rely on semantic cues and struggle with the geometric evidence needed for this task. - **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34037v1)

- **Authors**: Chaitat Utintu, Ahmed Bourouis, Arkaprabha Basu, Yi-Zhe Song
- **Comment**: Accepted in NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LVLM benchmarks tell us whether a model answers correctly, but not which visual conventions it has retained, nor where those conventions live. Sketches expose this hidden structure. A sketch removes texture, lighting, and photographic context while preserving the strokes humans judged sufficient for recognition. If an LVLM can operate on that abstraction, the surviving signal is structural rather than merely photometric. We introduce a training-free sketch probe with three readouts: temporal part-construction order, spatial attention-based grounding, and the mechanistic layer/head locus of grounding. We evaluate nine LVLMs on part order and five on the attention readouts, over two part-annotated sketch datasets. Three findings emerge. Second, part-level grounding survives the abstraction: attention heads selected from the frozen model with an existing recipe recover part and object regions on object and scene sketches, with no grounding supervision. Third, the grounding signal concentrates in a narrow mid-network band whose location follows the base language model rather than parameter count in the two families we compare, and remains stable across object and scene sketches. The result is a practical diagnostic for sketch-driven LVLMs: no fine-tuning, no auxiliary segmenter, and a layer band that doubles as a head-selection prior, matching or improving grounding mAcc@0.1 in every model and dataset we tested.

- **Summary**: Safe indoor operation requires autonomous tracking of objects that are not directly visible to the robot. Optical non-line-of-sight (NLoS) methods depend on ambient lighting and scene geometry or on weak multi-bounce returns that need long acquisitions. - **Summary**: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models map a language instruction and the current observation directly to actions, without checking whether the instruction has been completed. Moreover, the unfinished steps are reusable atomic skills that recur across demonstrations. A failed attempt can thus be recovered by composing with atomic skills.
