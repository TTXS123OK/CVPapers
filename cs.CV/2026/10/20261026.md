- **Comment**: None

- **Summary**: Background The combination of hematoma location, expansion and final volume influence neurological outcome. The aim of this study was to derive and validate a quantitative and interpretable region-based NCCT image feature model for predicting hematoma expansion. Methods The Ethnic/Racial Variations of Intracerebral Hemorrhage(ERICH) study was used for derivation and the Virtual International Trials in Stroke Archive(VISTA) was used for validation. NCCT images at presentation and 24+/-6hours were pre-processed, segmented and categorized into lobar, basal ganglia or thalamic regions. Hematoma expansion was defined by region and according to its effect on clinical outcome. Transport-based morphometry (TBM) produced a unique transport-based representation from each NCCT hematoma image. Linear classifiers applied to transport representations were used to derive a predictive model. The regional TBM model's performance was compared to alternate methods using AUROC analyses. The validation dataset included 196 patients, comprising 31 lobar, 119 basal ganglia and 46 thalamic hematomas. A combined regional model predicted hematoma expansion in the validation dataset with an AUROC of 0.787. The principal image features which varied by location were size, heterogeneity, eccentricity and density distribution towards the hematoma periphery. The regional TBM model outperformed alternative clinician based and machine-learning methods. Conclusions TBM discriminated and visualized regional features of hematoma expansion from presentation NCCT scans. This provides preliminary evidence for regional radiographic features of hematoma expansion. It has the potential to improve hematoma expansion prediction and understanding of hematoma expansion pathophysiology.

### Video World Models Need Predictable Latents

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34663v1)

- **Authors**: Mattia Scardecchia, Sherry Yang
- **Comment**: 54 pages, 25 figures, 14 tables. Code: https://github.com/mattia-scardecchia/predictable-latents
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Video world models trained on large-scale robot data promise a safe and scalable approach for learning and evaluating policies that act in the physical world. However, they still struggle with long-horizon rollouts and out-of-distribution generalization, and they remain computationally demanding at inference time. Typically, video world models make predictions in the latent space of a reconstruction-trained autoencoder, which however is not directly optimized for dynamics modeling. On the other hand, world models designed for latent planning usually optimize latents to be predictable, but they forgo accurate prediction of observations in pixel space. In this paper, we show that it is possible to achieve the best of both worlds: a latent geometry optimized to make future prediction as simple as possible without sacrificing accurate prediction of future observations. More precisely, we find that temporal objectives for representation learning, paired with a past-conditioned decoder architecture, lead to consistent improvements in generative video world models at the same training budget. The gains include up to 5x cheaper inference, over 7x faster training, and 15-24% lower pixel and perceptual errors out of distribution and over long horizons. Our world models also agree more closely with real-world policy evaluation when using a vision-language model to judge task success from their pixel-space predictions. Overall, our study indicates that temporal predictability is an important design criterion for the latent space of video world models, and provides concrete guidance on how to incorporate it into the training of autoencoders.

### Exploring perceptual straightness in learned visual representations

- **Authors**: Anne Harrington, Vasha DuTell, Ayush Tewari, Mark Hamilton, Simon Stent, Ruth Rosenholtz, William T. Freeman
- **Comment**: Published at ICLR 2023
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Humans have been shown to use a "straightened" encoding to represent the natural visual world as it evolves in time (Hénaff et al. 2019). In the context of discrete video sequences, "straightened" means that changes between frames follow a more linear path in representation space at progressively deeper levels of processing. While deep convolutional networks are often proposed as models of human visual processing, many do not straighten natural videos. In this paper, we explore the relationship between network architecture, differing types of robustness, biologically-inspired filtering mechanisms, and representational straightness in response to time-varying input; we identify strengths and limitations of straightness as a useful way of evaluating neural network representations. We find that (1) adversarial training leads to straighter representations in both CNN and transformer-based architectures but (2) this effect is task-dependent, not generalizing to tasks such as segmentation and frame-prediction, where straight representations are not favorable for predictions; and nor to other types of robustness. In addition, (3) straighter representations impart temporal stability to class predictions, even for out-of-distribution data. Finally, (4) biologically-inspired elements increase straightness in the early stages of a network, but do not guarantee increased straightness in downstream layers of CNNs. We show that straightness is an easily computed measure of representational robustness and stability, as well as a hallmark of human representations with benefits for computer vision models.

- **Summary**: Language-Only Vision-Language Model Selection (LOVM) aims to identify the Vision-Language Model (VLM) with the highest zero-shot accuracy on a target task using only textual information. - **Authors**: Amit Aflalo, Eran Treister, Chaim Baskin, Oren Freifeld
- **Comment**: Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Diffeomorphisms provide a flexible, topology-preserving mathematical tool for modeling complex spatial or temporal deformations and are used in various scientific fields. However, making these deformations simultaneously highly expressive and computationally efficient remains challenging. Continuous Piecewise-Affine Based (CPAB) transformations (Freifeld et al., ICCV 2015; IEEE TPAMI 2017) offer an attractive solution by parameterizing a family of diffeomorphisms via continuous velocity fields that are piecewise affine with respect to a chosen tessellation of the domain. Importantly, $d=\dim(θ)$, the dimension of the parameter vector $θ$, depends on the fineness of the tessellation rather than on the data resolution. Despite this compact parameterization, CPAB optimization has long been hindered by the tight coupling between trajectory integration and gradient computation with respect to $θ$. We introduce FG-CPAB, a factorized-gradient formulation for scalable CPAB transformations. By separating integration from parameter-space projection, our method reduces gradient computation complexity from $\mathcal{O}(dTN)$ to $\mathcal{O}(TN+dC)$, where $T$ is the number of integration steps, $N$ is the number of transformed points, and $C$ is the number of tessellation cells. This reformulation yields speedups of up to four orders of magnitude (e.g., $\approx 10^4\times$ in 2D), substantial memory savings (e.g., $\approx 500\times$ reduction in peak GPU memory usage in 2D), and dramatically higher expressiveness. This makes fine-tessellation CPAB practical in 2D and 3D for the first time, enabling highly expressive parametric diffeomorphisms.

- **Summary**: 4D novel view synthesis (NVS) aims to render dynamic scenes across viewpoints and timestamps, which requires jointly inferring 3D structure and temporal dynamics. Existing monocular feed-forward methods often rely on explicit 4D Gaussian representations, which are prone to temporal inconsistency and severe rendering artifacts. We present FL4N, the first feed-forward latent 4D NVS model. Instead of relying on explicit 4D primitives, FL4N encodes a 4D scene prior into a unified latent representation across space and time. - **Summary**: Do vision models encode the transition $2{\to}3$ the same way whether it appears in handwritten digits or in hand gestures, and do they "understand" that clean${\to}$dirty is the same transformation regardless of whether it is applied to a kitchen or a carpet? The linear representation hypothesis (LRH) answers this for language models: concept changes correspond to directions in (language) embedding space. In vision, the same concept can manifest in visually unrelated "regimes" that rarely co-occur: images of digits and hands do not share much in common in pixel space. To understand whether vision models still encode the underlying abstract concepts, we study 46 vision encoders across architectures, training objectives, and scales, on 10 concept families including numerosity, age, object identity, and MIT-States attributes. Within each regime, we find statistically significant concept directions near-universally across our tested models. However, two distinct regimes do not always share the same concept direction. Several models learn multiple linear structures for the same concept transition, split across regimes. We call this phenomenon "fragmented linearity": strong local linearity coexisting with near-absent global linearity. Fragmentation is, however, not fixed: models with stronger local linearity progressively escape it (local-global linearity correlated with $τ\in [0.38, 0.89]$ across all concepts), and where global linearity does emerge, concept directions can transfer as linear probes across regimes.

- **Summary**: Diffusion video models produce striking clips, but they cannot reliably render an exact formula, a correct chart, a ruleconsistent game or narration synchronized to on-screen events. A different route is emerging: multimodal agents that write code to render every frame and synthesize every sound. We introduce VibeVideoBench, a benchmark for evaluating agents on code-driven video generation. Its 239 self-contained tasks in English and Chinese span 12 categories, from data stories and music-driven pieces to classroom lessons and paper explainers; each asks an agent, running in its native harness inside a sandbox, to deliver a finished MP4 with sound within one hour. Evaluation is hierarchical and evidence-based: program checks of the delivery spec, about 13k task-specific rubric checks traced to the requirements they enforce, and five quality dimensions anchored per task, judged by three VLM judge panels and validated with blind human preferences. Across nine frontier agent systems and 2,151 scored rollouts, the benchmark is far from solved: GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 lead clearly, yet even the best system fails a core requirement on about a quarter of tasks. Whether a video arrives at all matters as much as its quality, and three quarters of the lost videos are final renders started too late, run too slowly or never started. The judges agree with each other and with human preferences on system rankings, but only moderately on individual videos.
