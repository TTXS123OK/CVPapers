### Meaningful Moments: A Corpus of VLM-Derived Temporal Importance Labels for Video Action Recognition
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16009v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16009v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-12 00:18:12+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-12 00:18:12+00:00
- **Authors**: Cameron Keith, SouYoung Jin
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 5 figures. Submitted to WACV 2027. Supplementary material included
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Not every moment in a video is equally useful for recognizing an action. A few decisive moments often carry the evidence: the instant an object moves, the peak of a dive, or one object placed on another. Existing video datasets rarely provide large-scale, action-conditioned labels for which short segments carry that evidence. This work asks whether vision-language models can identify meaningful moments at scale and whether those labels improve action recognition.We introduce Meaningful Moments (MM), a corpus of about 4.58 million VLM-generated per-segment importance scores for nearly 500,000 class-labeled videos spanning Something-Something v2 (SSv2), Kinetics-400 (K400), and Diving-48 across 622 action classes. We freeze the action classifier and vary only temporal sampling. An alpha-parameterized comparison of importance-led selection against an importance-inverted control separates gains from the VLM signal from gains caused by variable-density sampling alone. Across all three datasets, VLM-derived importance is informative: at the hard-cut endpoint, keeping VLM-important segments outperforms keeping VLM-designated filler. Usefulness depends on class structure. On Diving-48, importance-led fast-forwarding preserves full-video recognition while the inverted control collapses by roughly 30 percentage points. On K400, hard-cut selection beats uniform sampling by 1.45 percentage points, rising to 2.05 under threshold selection. On SSv2, the labels carry signal but do not recover full-video performance. Direct scoring matches greedy removal within its noise floor at roughly 25 times fewer oracle calls, and a four-VLM cross-oracle study shows consistent downstream trends. We release the importance labels and evaluation protocol under CC-BY-4.0; SSv2 class strings remain under CC-BY-NC-4.0.



### Adversarial Robustness of Camera-Radar Sensor Fusion for Autonomous Driving
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16012v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CR**, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16012v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-12 00:21:21+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-12 00:21:21+00:00
- **Authors**: Ce Zhou, Yubo Wu, Khang Nguyen, Liyang Xiao, Thomas Hou, Qiben Yan
- **Comment**: 25 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Autonomous driving (AD) relies on multi-sensor perception to interpret the surrounding environment. However, the robustness of camera--radar fusion under adversarial conditions remains largely unexplored. This paper presents a systematic measurement study of the robustness of camera--radar sensor fusion in AD. We construct a unified evaluation pipeline on the nuScenes dataset that simulates attacks on both sensing modalities, including eight camera-image perturbations and three radar point-cloud perturbations. We evaluate five camera--radar fusion algorithms across two perception tasks, 3D object detection and depth estimation, and quantify their performance under clean and attacked inputs using standard metrics. Our evaluation reveals a clear asymmetry in the robustness of fusion systems. Camera perturbations substantially degrade both object detection and depth estimation, and moderate attack strengths are already sufficient to cause severe performance degradation. In contrast, the evaluated radar perturbations produce smaller and task-dependent effects, with object-detection models showing particularly limited degradation. Our analysis attributes this difference to the strong dependence of existing fusion architectures on dense camera features, together with the filtering, association, and confidence-weighting mechanisms commonly applied to sparse radar measurements. These results demonstrate that sensor fusion does not inherently guarantee robustness and that the contribution and reliability of each sensing modality must be explicitly considered under adversarial conditions. Based on four empirical findings, we derive measurement-guided design implications for future robust camera--radar fusion, including modality-specific reliability estimation, cross-modal consistency checking, adaptive fusion, and graceful failover under severe sensor degradation.



### Removing the Compute Bottleneck for Event-based Visual Place Recognition with Spiking Neural Networks
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16022v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16022v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-12 00:45:01+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-12 00:45:01+00:00
- **Authors**: Somayeh Hussaini, Tobias Fischer, Michael Milford
- **Comment**: 8 pages, 6 figures, under review
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Neuromorphic sensing and computing promise low latency and high energy efficiency for robotics, yet most event-based visual place recognition (VPR) pipelines reintroduce dense, frame-like computation downstream of an inherently sparse sensor, forfeiting the sensor's latency advantage. In this paper, we address the problem of resolving an event-based VPR query in tens of microseconds on a commodity CPU. We train a two-layer spiking neural network (SNN) with additive spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP), which drives the learned weight matrix towards a strongly bimodal distribution, amenable to aggressive magnitude pruning. At inference, our pipeline exploits this sparsity by reading only the pruned weights corresponding to the query's active pixels. As surviving weights share a near-uniform magnitude, each neuron's score reduces to an integer count of matching synapses instead of a weighted sum, lowering per-query latency. This computation is small enough to remain resident in a CPU's fast on-chip cache. On three event-based VPR benchmarks, our sparse SNN exceeds the recall of LENS, an existing event-based SNN, while reducing per-query latency by 5.5-6.2x to tens of microseconds. It matches the recall of a dense variant of our network and of the sum-of-absolute-differences (SAD) baseline, while reading 10x fewer weights than the dense variant. We further characterise how recall and latency scale with the number of active pixels and reference places. These results suggest that the sparsity of both event-based input and learned weights can already be exploited for low-latency inference on conventional processors.

- **Summary**: Hour-long videos, streaming feeds, and movie-length content are pushing vision-language models (VLMs) past their breaking point -- the standard recipe of encoding each frame into visual tokens yields sequences that grow linearly with video length, causing prohibitive memory consumption and inference latency. We propose FramePack-U, a strikingly simple, training-free frame packing framework that compresses arbitrarily long videos into a fixed size visual representation at constant memory cost. - **Authors**: Hui Shan, Keyang Luo, Binjia Zhou, Ming Li, Kai Li, Xin Wang, Zhenyang Li, Zesong Yang, Weikai Chen, Runze Zhang, Xin Wang, Yanwei Fu, Zhen Chen, Xiangru Huang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: We present Unravel, a training-free framework that turns a single image into an interactive 3D scene, where every object is an independent and movable asset. Existing generators either produce one entangled geometry in which no object can be moved, or predict all objects in one feed-forward pass, where a wrong guess cannot be detected or repaired. Unravel instead takes a different approach: it first takes the scene apart, and then puts it back together. Starting from the input image, an agentic harness that iteratively peels the image layer by layer, and adopts point cloud as major modality for spatial relationship recovery. Specifically, a vision-language model plans which objects are fully visible, an image editor removes them and restores the occluded regions. Every edit is verified before being incorporated into the reconstruction. This process gradually turns occluded parts of the scene into direct observation, allowing objects to be reconstructed when they are best exposed rather than hallucinated from the original image alone. The scene is then rebuilt in reverse removal order, so that the supporting objects are naturally placed before the objects they support. Since a single-view image only reveals part of an object, we treat the observed points as a lower bound on its size: an asset may grow to cover its points, but can not shrink away from them. Finally, we physically settle the reconstructed scene to remove floating objects and interpenetrations, followed by an agentic audit that corrects remaining placement errors. Extensive experiments on diverse indoor scenes show that Unravel recovers more objects, places them more faithfully, and produces scenes that are more physically-plausible than existing methods.
