### Direct Conditional Parameterization for N-Dimensional Splatting
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22778v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22778v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-16 00:39:55+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 00:39:55+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhongpai Gao, Benjamin Planche, Van Nguyen Nguyen, Meng Zheng, Anwesa Choudhuri, Arun Innanje, Terrence Chen, Ziyan Wu
- **Comment**: Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: N-dimensional splatting extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with view direction and time to model view-dependent and dynamic effects. Each primitive is lifted into a joint distribution over position and conditioning variables, then conditionally sliced at each rendering query. N-DGS and UBS share this step across Gaussian and Beta kernels: recovering the conditional mean, opacity, and covariance requires per-primitive matrix inversions and regression products in every forward pass. We introduce direct conditional parameterization, replacing this covariance-derived readout with explicit per-effect parameters: a Cholesky precision factor for opacity and a factorized query-to-position regression operator with learnable per-dimension coupling. Unrestricted conditional coordinates recover a valid joint covariance; our factorization adds spatial-scale and coupling priors. Applied to both kernels, the parameterization yields dGS (Gaussian) and dBS (Beta). Across five static and dynamic benchmarks, they match or exceed their covariance-derived counterparts on quality, with gains up to +1.26 dB in the main MCMC evaluation. Production slicing is 6.9-7.7 times faster than on-the-fly covariance-derived conditioning; complete-render profiling gives 1.25-1.91 times speedups on four trained scenes. Direct conditioning provides a structured, kernel-agnostic alternative for N-dimensional splatting. Project page: https://gaozhongpai.github.io/dgs/.



### STRIDE: Evaluating Spatiotemporal Reasoning in Driving Edge Cases
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22795v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22795v1)

- **Authors**: Lieqi Liu, Rui gao, Jia-Chen Gu, Wenbo Hu, Zhaobin Mo, Ahmadreza Moradipari, Nejib Ammar, Wei Wang, Nanyun Peng
- **Comment**: Project page: https://pluslabnlp.github.io/STRIDE/
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: In autonomous driving, perceiving surrounding objects is not enough: failing to understand how they move and interact over time can lead to dangerous decisions. Evaluating vision-language models (VLMs) therefore requires testing whether they connect past spatial observations to future consequences. Yet existing benchmarks often assess edge cases through basic perception questions or test temporal understanding in simple scenarios, leaving complex spatiotemporal reasoning underexplored. We introduce STRIDE (SpatioTemporal Reasoning In Driving Edge Cases), an object-centric benchmark for long-tail, safety-critical driving scenarios. STRIDE covers six task families: spatial perception, spatial understanding, temporal memory, temporal extrapolation, trajectory prediction, and scene-context awareness. It pairs multiple-choice questions probing individual spatiotemporal judgments with related free-form visual question answering (VQA) requiring detailed scene explanations and justifications of driving decisions. We prioritize scenes with unusual object behaviors and complex traffic interactions, generate reference answers from structured dataset annotations through automated procedures, and use limited language-model assistance and human verification to ensure annotation quality. Across 12 proprietary, open-weight, and driving-specialized models, even the best performer, GPT-6-Astra, achieves only 43.4% average multiple-choice accuracy, versus an approximately 20% random-choice baseline. Further analysis reveals that strong performance in describing driving scenes does not necessarily translate to accurate safety-critical judgments, and that models struggle more with reconstructing object motion than recalling past positions. STRIDE thus exposes spatiotemporal driving limitations that aggregate VQA scores can conceal.

- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Speed-limit sign recognition on an automotive camera system-on-chip (SoC) must fit a detector and a classifier (the reader) into the memory, arithmetic, and energy budgets of the device. In our two-stage recognizer, the detector, a YOLO-family network with ReLU and hard-sigmoid operators, is compressed by channel replay: an iterative pruning cascade identifies the channels to remove, the recorded deletions are replayed on the original task-trained network, the parent, and one continuous knowledge-distillation recovery follows. Trained on public data only, the combined system of 0.72 M parameters recognizes 95.2% of the signs of a held-out automotive camera at 90% precision and 94 frames per second.

- **Summary**: Visual question answering (VQA) models are known to suffer from the bias issue, which refers to overfitting the regularities of frequent patterns. Previously, researchers only consider the language bias. Recently, the multimodal bias is noticed. However, what multimodal bias is remains unclear. In this paper, we investigate the multimodal bias in counting and non-counting questions. We find that they have distinct underlying mechanism, so their biases come from distinct reasons. We also provide explanation for the compromised performance on VQAv2.

- **Summary**: Vision-language models (VLMs) such as CLIP retain zero-shot recognition ability, but their open-world use often requires task-agnostic continual learning: tasks arrive sequentially, past data are unavailable, task identity is unknown, and the backbone cannot be retrained. We argue that this setting fails VLMs through three coupled pressures beyond the stability-plasticity tradeoff: (1) modal-consistency drift, (2) representation-space interference, and (3) per-task capacity shortfall. COMET shows that continual vision-language adaptation can preserve multimodal geometry, coordinate accumulated experts, and allocate capacity jointly when feature reference, expert shaping, routing, and capacity control are separated by construction.
