### Test-time Adaptation for Point Cloud Denoising under Unseen Noise
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27410v1
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.cad.2026.104182
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.GR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27410v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 00:04:09+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 00:04:09+00:00
- **Authors**: Kun Jiang, Shida Fu, Fanhao Yi, Weiqing Li, Zhiyong Su
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: Computer-Aided Design, 203: 104182, 2027
- **Summary**: Although deep learning-based point cloud denoising methods have demonstrated impressive results in recent years, their effectiveness usually degrades substantially in real-world scenarios where the noise distribution of test point clouds deviates from that of the training set. To address this challenge, we propose a test-time adaptive denoising (TTAD) framework that enables existing denoising approaches to adapt to unknown noise patterns at test time without relying on additional supervision or retraining from source data. Taking a pre-trained denoising model and a test noisy point cloud as input, TTAD first employs a residual-driven noise modeling (RDNM) module to estimate the sample-specific noise distribution through analyzing the deviation between the noisy input and its pseudo-clean version denoised by the initial model. Then, a noise-aware contrastive adaptation (NACA) module is designed to build sample-specific noisy-noisier test-time training pair from the denoised point cloud, and update the model parameters over a few adaptation steps per sample at test time through contrastive learning. Furthermore, TTAD naturally extends to a continual TTAD (CTTAD) setting, facilitating efficient progressive refinement across sequential test samples with consistent noise distributions, while reducing the computational cost per sample. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the generalization ability of existing denoising models under unseen noise, achieving superior performance across multiple evaluation metrics.

### TokenFlow: Latent Token Rectified Flow Matching for 3D Point Cloud Generation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27418v1
- **DOI**: None

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27418v1)

- **Authors**: Linhao Wang, Dongjin Huang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: 3D point clouds generation requires capturing both part-level layout and surface detail while preserving permutation equivariance. Existing latent generative models often encode an entire shape into a single vector, which limits their ability to retain part-level structure. We propose TokenFlow, a hierarchical flow matching framework that represents each shape as a set of latent tokens and couples a rectified flow over these tokens with a conditional flow over points. A bottleneck of $M \ll N$ learnable tokens aggregates global context through cross-attention at $O(NM)$ cost. These results show that representing a shape as a set of latent tokens, aggregated at $O(NM)$ cost, provides an effective representation for 3D point cloud generation.



### Using Set Learning to Improve Set-Valued Classifiers on Long-tailed Data
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27443v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27443v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 00:46:14+00:00
- **Authors**: Kevin Kasa, Scott C. Lowe, Graham W. Taylor
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Set-valued classification (SVC) offers a practical approach to communicating uncertainty while maintaining accuracy by predicting variable-sized sets per input rather than fixed-sized sets (e.g. top-1 or top-5). However, set-valued classifiers (SVCs) exhibit significantly degraded performance on long-tailed and imbalanced datasets, a setting for which they are ostensibly well-suited. This becomes apparent under macro-average evaluation, which weighs all classes equally. SVCs are typically formed during a post-hoc calibration stage, so little is known about how training dynamics affect their behaviour. In this work, we investigate how different neural network training strategies and loss functions impact downstream SVC performance on highly imbalanced data. In addition to methods such as label smoothing and class-balancing, we investigate whether loss functions that operate over sets of examples in a batch, rather than treating all examples as independent, lead to better downstream SVCs. We adapt the recently proposed odd-K-out (OKO) loss function, which learns from sets of training samples, improving its sample efficiency whilst introducing algorithmic modifications that reduce its computational overhead from 3.2x to just 1.36x that of standard cross-entropy training. Experiments on highly long-tailed datasets demonstrate our modified OKO approach significantly improves macro-accuracy across multiple SVC methods - including top-k, average-k, and conformal prediction - while maintaining competitive micro-accuracy. This improved performance on minority classes occurs without substantially increasing prediction set sizes, suggesting that carefully constructed set-based training can produce networks better suited to imbalanced data.



### LoRASpace: A Pool-Wide Shared Substrate for Static and Dynamic Multi-LoRA Composition
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27444v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27444v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 00:46:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 00:46:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Thamirawaran Sathiyalogeswaran, Navindu De Silva, Chamikara Siriwardane, W. K. Alwis, Jason Mars, Udaya Sampath K. Perera Miriya Thanthrige, Ranga Rodrigo
- **Comment**: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 29 pages, 6 figures, 26 tables. Code: https://github.com/Thamirawaran/loraspace. Project page: https://thamirawaran.github.io/loraspace
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) attaches small low-rank matrices to a frozen backbone, and public repositories host many LoRAs per backbone, each for a different concept or task. Combining several LoRAs in one generation forces a trade-off. Merging their weights into one update needs a single forward pass, but LoRAs trained for different concepts interfere and quality drops. Running each LoRA in its own forward pass (composite) preserves quality, but inference time grows linearly with the number of active LoRAs. We study a property of LoRA pools overlooked by prior multi-LoRA work. In real LoRA pools, the stacked low-rank factors need far fewer directions than their summed ranks. Using singular value decomposition, we find the minimal subspace that keeps 99% of the factor energy. On three diffusion pools, only 46% to 57% of the summed LoRA ranks is needed, and the subspace grows sub-linearly with pool size. A 48-LoRA sub-pool of LoRA-Hub on FLAN-T5 tasks shows comparable overlap (62%), while the diversity-curated LoRA-Retriever benchmark sits near the no-overlap baseline (96%), so pool composition rather than modality predicts where compression is large. We build on this overlap with LoRASpace, a training-free method that computes the subspace once per pool and reuses it in two inference modes, a static mode at merge cost and a dynamic mode for input-dependent routing. On the diffusion pools, static LoRASpace matches composite quality at merge cost, and dynamic LoRASpace is more than 6x faster than composite with six active LoRAs on a 17-LoRA pool, mainly because it runs one backbone pass instead of six. In an ablation, static variants at the same rank match LoRASpace, so the quality gain does not depend on the pool-wide basis. On the near-orthogonal LoRA-Retriever benchmark, compression is small and gains come from coupling with external retrieval.

- **Summary**: Pretrained Vision-Language Models (PVLMs) achieve strong zero-shot recognition but often suffer performance degradation under target-domain shift. Unsupervised adaptation provides a practical way to improve target-domain performance without labeled data, but it remains challenging because pseudo-labels generated by the model can be unreliable and no labeled validation set is available for model selection or early stopping. We propose a simple framework for unsupervised PVLM adaptation that combines progressive margin-based subset selection with cascaded consistency learning.
