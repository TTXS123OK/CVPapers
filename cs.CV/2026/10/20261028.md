### From Next Day Wildfire to Next Days Wildfire: A New Multi-Continent Dataset of Active-Fire Spread for Sequence-to-Sequence Forecasting

- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38086v1)

- **Authors**: Jose Guillen, Renzo Zanca, Valentin Barriere
- **Comment**: Accepted at WACV 2027. 19 pages including supplementary material, 12 figures, 13 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Benchmarks such as WildfireSpreadTS (WSTS) have enabled data-driven forecasting of wildfire spread from multimodal satellite time series, but they suffer from limitations: size restricts them to large fires that burn for weeks, and they are from similar environments. We introduce AFSeq, a multimodal dataset of 1,194 recent fire events (2023 to 2026) from 8 different countries across three regions, dominated by the short, small fires that existing benchmarks underrepresent. Our dataset is the largest and most diverse to date for time-series-based wildfire spread prediction. We also introduce UTAE-AR, an extension of a classical U-Net with Temporal Auto Encoder, enhanced with recurrent modules before the decoder. This allows the model to not only predict the next day but the whole sequence in an autoregressive way. Moreover, our model can feed its own predicted fire mask back one day at a time, producing the day-by-day forecasts that single-step models cannot. Our results show that UTAE-AR surpasses state-of-the-art on both WSTS, WSTS+ and AFSeq for next-day accuracy while having 21.5% of their parameters, and being able to predict confidently at a longer horizon.

- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 00:46:34+00:00
- **Authors**: Maris Basha, Yuhang Wang, Xiaoran Chen, Longbiao Cheng, Luca Yapura, Anja T. Zai, Mathieu Salzmann, Richard Hahnloser
- **Comment**: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Evaluations and Datasets Track). 43 pages, 3 figures. Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/songbirdini/v33da ; Code: https://github.com/marisbasha/v33da
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Deciding which member of an animal group produced a vocalization and where that vocalization was produced are central to studying social communication but are rarely evaluated with physically verified ground truth. Existing resources either localize sounds without identifying the caller, or recognize individuals from isolated vocalizations without reliance on the 3D candidate geometry. We close this gap with V33DA, a dataset-centered multimodal benchmark for spatial vocal attribution in social zebra finches: it comprises 33,625 verified events coupling 5-channel audio with three camera views, calibrated 3D pose for every visible candidate, and per-bird radio telemetry across 10 individuals and three experiments. Caller identity is verified from an on-body accelerometer-derived vibration signal; this channel is withheld from benchmark models and used only by an on-body reference ceiling. Given a detected vocalization and the visible candidate birds, the task is to select the caller. Reference methods expose a consistent gap between in-domain accuracy and transfer to unseen individuals: methods that can exploit familiar vocal identity perform well on known callers but degrade sharply on unseen ones, whereas candidate-aware methods preserving explicit spatial reasoning transfer substantially better. The on-body reference stays near-perfect across regimes, confirming the internal consistency of the accelerometer-verified labels. We release V33DA with fixed protocols and code for the evaluated methods, and V33DA++, an unevaluated companion set of verified overlapping-call events and +/-2 s context windows.

- **Authors**: Adityan Thirumani Srinivasan
- **Comment**: 11 pages, 5 figures, 8 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LOL-v1 and LOLv2-Real are the standard benchmarks for supervised low-light image enhancement, and several leading methods ship hand-set test-time adjustments with their evaluation scripts. We audit both benchmarks with 18 public checkpoints of eight methods under one metric implementation that reproduces every published number to within 0.09 dB, and find that their test labels differ from their training labels. (i) The 15 LOL-v1 test labels are rendered differently: the same captures appear in LOLv2 with labels of identical luminance but 1.48x lower saturation, and LOL-v1's own training labels match the LOLv2 rendering; we confirm this on the official release. (ii) The LOLv2-Real test set is the contiguous block of images 690-789, whose labels are 14% darker than the training labels (Cliff's delta = -0.45, p < 1e-12) with no comparable shift in the input statistics we examined; methods trained without any labels score up to 5.5 dB higher on test than on train. Separating what can be calibrated on training data from what only test-set tuning finds, the test-only residual has median 0.00 dB on LOLv2-Syn, which has no shift, but 0.37 dB on LOL-v1 and 0.92 dB on LOLv2-Real (up to 3.9 dB). Under the shift, test-set checkpoint selection inflates a from-scratch model by 0.9-2.0 dB, and removing the released settings changes which method ranks first. We give concrete reporting recommendations.

- **Comment**: WACV 2027, 8 main pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Controllable human motion generation has largely focused on specifying what action should occur or where the body should move. We study a complementary question: can full-body motion be controlled by specifying only when each foot contacts the ground? We introduce StepControl, an autoregressive diffusion model conditioned on a future binary left/right foot-contact schedule, without requiring target root and body poses, or semantic prompts. StepControl represents contact as explicit temporal events and injects them through dedicated cross-attention with classifier-free guidance. Our results show that minimal foot-contact schedules provide an effective and intuitive temporal interface for controllable motion generation.



### REEL: Same Frames, Different Answers and Different Costs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38158v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38158v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-28 01:53:23+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 01:53:23+00:00
- **Authors**: Xin Liang, Jiangpeng He
- **Comment**: Accepted at the IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV) 2027, Datasets and Evaluation Track
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Video understanding keeps moving toward longer recordings, and the longer a recording is, the more questions can be asked about it. But processing it again for every question does not scale, so many systems watch the video once, build a memory, and answer later questions from it. Yet what to remember is decided before anyone knows what will be asked, and whatever is left out cannot be recovered later.   Moreover, whether the savings pay off is judged by token counts, which fall when the input gets shorter but not when the model reuses what it has already computed. We present REEL, an evaluation framework that compares three saved representations built from exactly the same observed frames, so that a change in answers can come only from what the system remembered. Text writes a short record of what happened, Visual keeps the encoder outputs, and Prefix State also keeps the language-model prefix. Each is compared with full reprocessing (Frames) by paired correctness changes, cumulative GPU time, and storage size. Across six vision-language models and two benchmarks, Text and Frames differ in correctness on 20\% to 29\% of questions, even when their mean accuracy is almost the same. By contrast, Visual and Prefix State stay much closer to Frames. With unlimited storage, Prefix State is the cheapest in total GPU time among memories that keep almost all of the answers Frames gets right, for every model on both benchmarks. The trade-off is storage: Prefix State keeps up to about 21 GiB per video, whereas Text needs only kilobytes. Reusing computation and discarding information both look like efficiency, but only the second removes evidence that later questions may need, and neither mean accuracy nor token count tells them apart. REEL makes that difference measurable for any system that answers many questions from one saved memory.



### Neighbourhood shortcuts in building damage assessment from very-high-resolution satellite imagery: diagnosis and a building-evidence plug-in
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38162v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38162v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-28 02:07:44+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 02:07:44+00:00
- **Authors**: JianChong Guo, YuTing Wan, Kefan Li, AiLong Ma, YanFei Zhong
- **Comment**: 22 pages, 9 figures, 11 tables. Code: https://github.com/SummerTide/neighbourhood-shortcut-bda
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Building damage assessment (BDA) from very-high-resolution imagery supports disaster response, and deep models now achieve high overall accuracy on public benchmarks. Because damage is spatially clustered, most buildings share the damage level of their neighbours, so overall scores are dominated by such buildings, whereas damaged buildings among intact ones are rare and easily overlooked. A high overall score therefore cannot tell whether a model judges the building itself or relies on its neighbourhood as a shortcut, and this study tests which of the two current models do. We stratify buildings by their agreement with their neighbours, move buildings into new neighbourhoods with their pixels unchanged, and diagnose nine models covering the main paradigms of BDA. All models miss a third to half of the isolated damaged buildings, and in non-flood events they call 73--87% of intact buildings damaged once these are moved into a damaged neighbourhood. We propose a building-evidence plug-in that adds two experts to an existing model without retraining it. One expert sees only the building, and the other sees its surroundings but is trained with seamlessly swapped neighbourhoods so that it learns not to trust them. With each of the nine models, the plug-in maintains or improves overall accuracy, reduces errors on buildings that disagree with their neighbours by 8--18%, and cuts by 40--69% the intact buildings that a damaged neighbourhood turns into damaged ones. It also raises the detection of synthetic isolated damage from 1--6% to 13--39% and carries over to optical--SAR data and to an unseen earthquake. High overall scores thus conceal a systematic reliance on the neighbourhood, which building-level evidence reduces at low cost.

- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Federated Learning (FL) allows for secure analysis of distributed data without data sharing. We and others have successfully applied FL to neuroimaging tasks, such as Alzheimer's disease classification from structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). FL trains a deep learning model locally at each site, encrypts the local model parameters and sends them to a federation controller that aggregates the local models under homomorphic encryption, the resulting community model is sent back to the sites for further training, and the process repeats for a number of rounds.
