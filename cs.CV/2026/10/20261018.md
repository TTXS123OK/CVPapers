### LowNet: Looped Wavelet Neural Networks with 24 Learnable Parameters for Solving Poisson Equations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24941v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.CV, cs.LG, eess.IV, math.AP, math.NA
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24941v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-18 01:26:28+00:00
- **Authors**: Yuanhao Gong
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Poisson equations frequently appear in various fields, such as mathematics, physics and informatics. Solving Poisson equation, however, is challenging. Traditional methods are not data-specific, although have high certainty. They require a large number of iterations due to the CFL numerical stability. In contrast, deep learning methods are data-driven and more efficient, but require accurate training data and suffer from heavy computation. To tackle these issues, we propose a wavelet-inspired Poisson solver with 24 trainable scalars. The proposed network has several advantages. First, it is compact. It has four independent $5\times5$ kernels, each represented by six parameters, leading to 24 scalars in total. Such compact format can be deployed on most devices. Second, thanks to the loop design, it achieves higher accuracy than previous methods. A residual loop reuses the wavelet block without increasing its parameters. Numerical experiments confirm that such loop indeed improves the solving accuracy. On 10000 images, one, two, and three loops of the wavelet block lead to PSNRs of $47.27$, $74.14$, and $100.74$ dB, respectively. Third, the proposed method is computationally fast. Thanks to the wavelet transform, the proposed method has linear computation complexity, leading to high computation performance. In short, we propose a novel looped wavelet neural network with compactness, accuracy and efficiency for solving Poisson equations. Our method can be applied in various fields, such as computational fluid dynamics, image reconstruction, biomedical engineering, electromagnetic field simulation.

- **Summary**: Different visual token pruning strategies retain different subsets while maintaining similar task performance. This motivates a combinatorial question: how many visual subsets can preserve model behavior, and how does their abundance depend on the query? At a fixed layer and budget, visual sufficiency probability is the fraction of uniformly sampled subsets that jointly preserve the dense model's answer, reference-answer likelihood, and output distribution. - **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25005v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-18 04:19:13+00:00

- **Authors**: Guangzong Si, Dong Wang, Yifan Yu, Miao Liu, Panwang Pan, Wentao Zhu
- **Comment**: Accepted to Findings of EMNLP 2026, forthcoming
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) encode images into dense visual token sequences, creating substantial attention and KV-cache overhead. Existing visual token compression methods reduce this cost by pruning low-saliency tokens in shallow layers, but this practice couples temporary computational deactivation with irreversible information deletion. We show that this coupling breaks an intrinsic inference pattern in MLLMs. Through layer-wise attention analysis, we identify \textbf{Visual Backtracking}: visual pivots remain spatially stable, yet in middle layers they actively retrieve context from tokens that appeared unimportant earlier. Thus, early low-saliency tokens can be inactive for computation while still being useful for later reasoning. Motivated by this finding, we propose \textbf{ReTrace}, a backtracking-aware compression framework that introduces a dormant token state. ReTrace caches non-pivot tokens outside expensive layer-wise computation, restores them during the backtracking window, and prunes only after their information has been absorbed. Experiments on Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3 demonstrate nearly 90\% reductions in FLOPs and KV cache, a 2.83$\times$ prefilling speedup, and strong performance retention across complex vision-language benchmarks.

- **Summary**: Real-time streaming video question answering with vision-language models must answer queries that arrive at any time with low latency while sustaining the video-processing rate required by the application. - **Summary**: Identifying which visual interventions interact in a vision-language model requires comparing their joint and separate effects. At each selected linear projection, FIL decomposes each candidate's input into a native state, two single-intervention components, and a full-dimensional mixed residual.
