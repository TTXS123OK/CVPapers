### Hexagonal Basis Projectors for Model-Based Iterative CT Reconstruction
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24363v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24363v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-17 01:27:01+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-17 01:27:01+00:00
- **Authors**: Ke Chen, Alireza Entezari
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Image representation affects both the forward model and quantitative feature recovery in model-based iterative CT reconstruction. For isotropically bandlimited signals, the hexagonal lattice is the most sampling-efficient lattice in two dimensions, with greater directional isotropy and a larger alias-free bandwidth than the Cartesian grid at equal density. We develop constant and linear hexagonal projectors for two-dimensional fan-beam CT using box spline calculus. Both bases form a partition of unity on common coefficient centers; the linear tent also reproduces affine functions. We derive analytic ray footprints and effective detector-blur expressions and implement efficient GPU forward and backprojection. On simulated FORBILD data, the linear model improves on Cartesian and hexagonal pixels, with its best noisy-data accuracy at the coarser resolution. Real-world 2DeteCT comparisons use high-dose, high-resolution Cartesian reconstructions as pseudo ground truth and show competitive image and feature agreement. These results establish a practical hexagonal representation for quantitative CT.

### PACE-Mem: Prefix-Aware Complementary Evidence for Multimodal Agents
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24378v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24378v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-17 02:24:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-17 02:24:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Shidu Ren, Rong-cheng Tu, Hang Zhou, Xiao Luo
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 5 figures. Code: https://github.com/Shidu-Ren/PACE-Mem
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: As multimodal agents accumulate experience, their long-term memories contain many similar observations of recurring scenes, actions, and entities. Answer quality depends on the evidence retrieved from these memories into the model's context. However, query-relevant memories may contain overlapping information, and a fixed retrieval depth may return too little or too much evidence for a given question. To address these challenges, we propose PACE-Mem, Prefix-Aware Complementary Evidence, which constructs a complementary evidence sequence and determines its length from the same selection trajectory. For structured memories, query-guided pooling combines records within each clip to obtain relevance scores and representations. Using these relevance scores and representations, maximal marginal relevance (MMR) selection balances query relevance against overlap with previously selected evidence. From the resulting score trajectory, a prefix-length selection algorithm determines retrieval depth by comparing length-normalized adjacent declines, accounting for both score changes and the amount of evidence accumulated. PACE-Mem is a training-free, plug-in retrieval method for clip-based agent memory systems, leaving memory construction and answer generation unchanged. Across three long-video benchmarks and two agent frameworks, PACE-Mem improves question-answering accuracy by an average of 1.6% over Adaptive-k, the strongest baseline at comparable mean retrieval depths. Further analysis reveals that jointly adding and removing evidence is associated with larger net accuracy gains than either change alone.



### Defending Against Dormant Poisoning Attacks Across Language and Multimodal Agents
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24379v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CR**, cs.CL, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24379v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-17 02:26:09+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-17 02:26:09+00:00
- **Authors**: Wonjun Lee, Vaidehi Patil, Zaid Khan, Yue Zhang, Hyunji Lee, Suhyun Kim, Mohit Bansal
- **Comment**: Project page: https://wonjuun.github.io/LookAhead/
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Recent work shows that attackers can implant dormant malicious behaviors that remain hidden at release but emerge after benign fine-tuning. As foundation models are used as agents, whether dormant poisoning extends to harmful agent actions and multimodal settings remains unclear. We show two agentic risks: (1) harmful behaviors activated by benign fine-tuning can extend to unseen actions; (2) multiple harmful behaviors can coexist and be jointly activated by benign downstream fine-tuning. Existing defenses often rely on external safe responses, which are hard to obtain for agent actions, or a trusted clean reference model. We exploit a key property of dormant poisoning: the attacker must preserve safe behavior at release to conceal poisoning, and this behavior can serve as a safety reference. Leveraging this property, we automatically construct a Safety Buffer pairing harmful inputs with the released model's responses and benign versions generated by the same model to remove harmful intent. The Safety Buffer can replace external safe responses in existing defenses, but these defenses do not distinguish updates that weaken safety from those that preserve it, often increasing over-refusal or reducing utility. To address this limitation, we introduce LookAhead Defense, which previews benign fine-tuning updates using the Safety Buffer and penalizes only updates predicted to weaken safe behavior on harmful inputs or increase the likelihood of refusals on benign versions in the Safety Buffer. Across language models, language agents, and multimodal agents, LookAhead Defense reduces harmful behavior in agents from up to 93.9% to at most 14.2% and harmful responses in language models from up to 92.5% to at most 2.0% versus vanilla fine-tuning, while maintaining competitive utility and low over-refusal. It remains effective against other poisoning methods.

- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: In multimodal learning, modality imbalance often lets one modality dominate optimization and keeps the model from fully exploiting cross-modal interactions. Balance-oriented methods such as the Multimodal Competition Regularizer (MCR) perform well by alleviating this imbalance during training. These dynamic balancing methods, however, mainly adjust modality contributions and do not consider how imbalance affects the formation of synergistic information during fusion. Synergy is a major source of multimodal advantage, because it is predictive gain that cannot be recovered from unimodal evidence alone. We propose the Multimodal Synergistic Competition Regularizer (MSCR), a fusion method that jointly promotes balanced modality utilization and the discovery of synergistic information. An adaptive gate further concentrates the regularization on samples that need stronger collaborative modeling. Experiments on five benchmarks show that MSCR consistently improves performance over MCR and strengthens synergistic capability. It raises conditional recovery, the accuracy of the fused prediction on samples that the unimodal predictors misclassify, as well as the share of the model's output that InterSHAP attributes to cross-modal interactions. Its gain in conditional recovery persists when the fusion module is Transformer-based. Code is available at https://github.com/chenxinyu991-collab/MSCR.

- **Summary**: We present Spatons, a family of compositional, spatially localized neural operators for computationally efficient XR (extended reality) predistortion and distortion compensation. - **Summary**: Semantic segmentation depends heavily on real images with pixel-level annotations, making dense supervision a major obstacle to deployment in new domains. - **Summary**: Online scene change detection requires an agent to detect what has changed in a previously mapped environment while it is still on site, observing from unconstrained viewpoints and without access to future observations. This causal setting is strictly harder than its offline counterpart, and online methods have consistently trailed offline accuracy. O-SCD closed that gap by treating change as a persistent property of a 3D Gaussian splatting map of the reference scene, surpassing the strongest offline methods in change detection accuracy while operating at over 10 frames per second. O-SCD optimizes a change field over the Gaussians of the reference map, refitting it to all past cue maps at every frame, a step that consumes more than half of the per-frame budget and forces accuracy to be traded against speed. The self-supervised objective that O-SCD minimizes by descent also determines the decision threshold exactly, by sorting, yielding a label-free per-frame cutoff whose margin provides a selective-prediction signal.
