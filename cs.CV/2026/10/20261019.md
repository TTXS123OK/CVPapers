- **Authors**: Muntaqim Ahmed Raju, Priyanka Siddappa, Md Shifat Haider Al Amin, Ruizhe Ma, Md Ripon Ahammed
- **Comment**: IEEE ICDM DMBIH 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: A central challenge in multimodal learning is how to fuse modalities that differ substantially in predictive strength. When one modality provides a much stronger representation than another, indiscriminate cross-modal interaction can introduce weak, redundant, or unstable signals, limiting or even degrading performance relative to the strongest unimodal representation. To address this problem, we propose CheXFuse, a multimodal fusion framework based on Guided Differential Joint Attention (GuDJA). CheXFuse combines image and text token representations within a joint multimodal sequence, where learned token guidance emphasizes informative representations and differential attention reduces less useful attention responses. We evaluate CheXFuse on the Indiana University (IU) Chest X-ray dataset, using binary (normal vs. abnormal) classification as the downstream task. Experiments are conducted under both original-report and masked-report settings, with the latter serving as a controlled stress test under reduced textual informativeness. CheXFuse with GuDJA achieves the strongest mean overall performance across the evaluated baselines and ablations, reaching 95.73% accuracy, 95.79% F1-score, 96.54% recall, and 95.28% sensitivity at 95% specificity (Sens@95Spec) with original reports, and 95.58%, 95.60%, 95.60%, and 94.34%, respectively, under masked reports. These results suggest that GuDJA provides a promising fusion strategy under asymmetric or partially reduced modality informativeness.

- **Summary**: Vision-based object pose estimation is vital for conducting spacecraft rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO). However, event-based spacecraft pose estimation (EBSPE) suffers when the relative motion between the chaser and target is slow, since the event rate drops significantly, leading to data with low signal-to-noise ratio. - **Summary**: Camera-based 3D semantic occupancy models can be trained without 3D labels by rendering their predictions into the images and supervising them with 2D pseudo labels. - **Summary**: Unsupervised cross-domain person re-identification remains challenging because domain shifts generate noisy pseudo labels that are reinforced during iterative adaptation. ### Clean Forcing: Drift-Resistant Autoregressive Video Diffusion with a Frozen Base

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25634v1)

- **Authors**: Wenqing Wang, Joonghyuk Shin, Jonathan Tremblay, Chan Hee Song, Yun Fu
- **Comment**: Project page: https://clean-forcing.github.io/
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models generate streaming video of arbitrary length by conditioning each new chunk of frames on those already generated. However, they suffer from exposure bias, where a model trained on clean context must continue from its own imperfect outputs, and errors compound until quality collapses within seconds (drift). Existing methods either require cluster-scale base retraining, apply training-free inference corrections with inconsistent results, or train frozen-base correctors for sampling and cache errors. We instead find that drift is largely deterministic and therefore learnable, as holding the noisy state fixed while varying only the history reveals a counterfactual velocity gap that is ~95% systematic across noise seeds. Based on this observation, we introduce Clean Forcing, which trains a 5.9M-parameter LoRA corrector on the frozen base model to regress this gap, with a closed-loop stage that adds DAgger-style aggregation and a drift-contraction objective to expose the corrector to its own rollouts. Because the clean histories can be the base model's own single-shot generations, training needs no real videos, and the merged corrector adds no inference cost. In 50 s text-to-video generation, Clean Forcing reduces Δ-drift from +11.3 to +1.65 without real video and to -0.16 with 40 real clips, improving on the best published result of +3.57 with over 150x less data. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Clean Forcing outperforms prior methods in aesthetic quality and data efficiency with reduced drift for long-horizon video generation.

- **Summary**: Medical image registration is a fundamental task for aligning anatomical structures across images. - **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25664v1)

- **Comment**: 37 pages, 13 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Diffusion and flow matching models achieve impressive visual generation quality but remain computationally expensive due to iterative sampling. Caching-based acceleration provides a practical training-free solution by reusing or predicting intermediate features. However, existing methods largely enforce a forward-only information flow: approximations produced for skipped model evaluations are immediately committed to the sampling trajectory and cannot benefit from feedback generated by later full-computation steps. We propose \textbf{PoSTCache} (\emph{Postdictive Smoothing Trajectory Cache}), a training-free and plug-and-play framework that reformulates diffusion caching as revisable trajectory inference. Experiments on representative image and video generation models show that PoSTCache consistently improves the quality--efficiency trade-off over representative caching methods.

- **Summary**: Streaming video world models generate the future chunk by chunk, conditioned on their own past and a stream of actions. Trained on short clips, they drift once a rollout exceeds the training horizon, as prediction errors re-enter the context and compound. A common remedy is to train the model on its own rollouts via distribution matching distillation (DMD). For action-conditioned world models, the teacher becomes a bottleneck: an external video model is agnostic to the actions, whereas the model's own pretrained network drifts in the same manner as the student. We observe that a long rollout need not match a teacher, provided it remains a realistic continuation of its own scene that follows the commanded actions. Realism and scene fidelity can be measured against real-data statistics, which remain valid at any horizon, while action following is preserved by the model's conditional generation loss. We propose Fréchet Forcing, which fine-tunes a world model together with its deployed multi-step sampler under three constraints. Generative anchoring applies the flow-matching loss after a self-generated history ending on real data. Context fidelity, a Fréchet distance (FD) on latent statistics of the free rollout centred on each clip's own ground truth, keeps every rollout faithful to its scene. Reality matching, an FD to real frames in a frozen perceptual space, keeps the generated frames realistic. Because all three targets are derived from short real clips without comparing a rollout against a long recorded future, training requires no teacher, no online critic and no long videos. Fine-tuning MiniWorld-1B, pretrained on 253-frame clips, for at most 36 GPU-hours on 381-frame clips yields rollouts that remain stable beyond 4,000 frames and continue to follow their actions in both robot manipulation and camera-controlled exploration.

### BitFlat: Error-Localized Bitstream Design for Robust Diffusion Image Compression

- **Summary**: Zero-shot diffusion image compression based on reverse channel coding achieves high perceptual quality at low bitrates without retraining, yet its bitstream remains vulnerable to bit flips. Prior work has shown that a joint combinatorial representation can amplify a single bit flip into substitutions of multiple atoms and that indexing each atom independently confines this error propagation. However, independent indexing reduces the number of atoms that fit within the bit budget and gives the decoder no indication of which atoms are corrupted. To address these issues, we propose BitFlat, a training-free error-localizing bitstream framework with two components. Blockwise Locality Alignment (BLA) decomposes the globally coupled atom selection at each diffusion step into independent selection units, each describing one signed atom from a dedicated codebook with a fixed-width field, so that any bit error affects only the unit in which it occurs. Reliability-Aware Diffusion Control (RADC) uses the bits saved by BLA to impose structured valid-codeword constraints within each field and converts the decoding state of each field into a binary reliability gate, which turns detected corrupted atoms into erasures at known positions and excludes them from noise synthesis. BitFlat requires neither target residuals nor additional network forward passes and leaves the diffusion model unchanged. Experiments on Kodak, DIV2K, and CLIC2020 show that BitFlat outperforms conventional, learned, and diffusion-based codecs at bit error rates from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$ and, even with $26.7\%$ fewer bits, surpasses Robust Turbo-DDCM.
