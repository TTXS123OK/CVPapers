### An Empirical Study on Learning Rooftop Solar Potential from Satellite Imagery Directly Without Explicit Geometry
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41111v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41111v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 01:09:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Joshua Lee
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Rooftop solar potential estimation often relies on LiDAR, digital surface models (DSMs), or pipelines that explicitly reconstruct roof geometry before estimating solar potential. We propose Direct Rooftop Solar Prediction (DRSP), an end-to-end formulation that maps satellite imagery and coarse regional sunlight information directly to application-level solar outputs. DRSP neither requires explicit geometric representations as input nor predicts roof orientation, slope, height, DSMs, or other geometric quantities as intermediate outputs, and therefore requires no geometric intermediate supervision. To enable large-scale study of this formulation, we develop a programmatic data-curation pipeline that converts per-panel information from the Google Solar API into usable-rooftop masks and spatial solar-energy targets, yielding two geographically distinct datasets containing approximately 60K rooftops from the U.S. Midwest and 87K from Texas. We demonstrate the feasibility of DRSP and systematically characterize the effects of geographic training distribution, model depth, image resolution, regional sunlight conditioning, and heatmap regression objectives. Overall, our results suggest that useful rooftop-scale solar information can be learned directly from 2D imagery without explicit geometric intermediate representations, offering a simpler path toward large-scale solar assessment. Our code and dataset are available at https://github.com/joshualee124/direct_rooftop_solar_prediction.



### CompoSET: A Controlled Single-Edit Benchmark for Vision-Language Compositionality
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41118v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41118v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-30 01:18:34+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 01:18:34+00:00
- **Authors**: Laure Delisle, Pietro Perona
- **Comment**: Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/CompoSET/CompoSET | Code: https://github.com/laure-delisle/CompoSET | 9 pages main text, 38 pages total
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Vision-language compositionality is the ability to identify which properties, relations, quantities, and states bind to which entities in both an image and a caption, and to match those bindings across modalities. Existing benchmarks evaluate this ability through image-text matching: a model ranks candidate image-caption match similarities and succeeds when the correct match comes on top. In such datasets, paired images differ by more than a single binding: scene, layout, and content all differ, with 97% of pixels changing on average. Such pairs do not isolate compositionality: results are confounded by global image differences. We introduce Compositional Single-EdiT (CompoSET), a benchmark dataset in which each base image-caption pair has an edited counterpart, or foil, that preserves the scene but differs by a single controlled edit, such as color, count, spatial relation, or object state. Base and foil images preserve naturalistic scene complexity yet are visually near-identical: only 8% of pixels change on average. Our dataset has many foils per base scene (~22) in order to separate scene difficulty from edit difficulty, a distinction one-foil-per-base benchmarks cannot make. CompoSET contains 1,776 instances across 80 naturalistic scenes, spanning 16 edit types in 4 semantic categories, with captions at 3 verbosity levels. We test 22 dual-encoder VLMs and find that all models perform at or below chance level on CompoSET, despite scoring well on prior benchmarks. This suggests that apparent progress under prior image-text matching protocols may be an artifact arising from confounds in benchmark design, and that compositional understanding in dual-encoder VLMs is far from solved. CompoSET addresses the confounds and offers a better controlled benchmark to measure future progress.

- **Summary**: Computer vision models learn hand action only from its visual consequences, without access to the underlying embodied motor signals. MotorSense pairs bimanual EMG bands with egocentric video and yields synchronized video, bimanual EMG and IMU, and audio across 100 hours of unscripted manipulation.
