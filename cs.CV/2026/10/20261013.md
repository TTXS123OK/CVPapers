### CÆNL: Collapse-Based Active Neural Learning with Objective-Aligned Representation Control
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17509v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17509v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-13 00:12:58+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-13 00:12:58+00:00
- **Authors**: Saad Alqithami
- **Comment**: Code and numerical reproduction: https://github.com/alqithami/CANL
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Active learning changes the representations used to acquire subsequent labels. We study this interaction through Collapse-Based Active Neural Learning (CÆNL), which separates acquisition, Dynamic Collapse Regularization (DCR), and its coefficient policy. DCR penalizes a normalized within-class to between-class scatter residual above a frozen reference, with a separate class-separation safeguard. Fixed, scheduled, deterministic-feedback, and learned-feedback policies apply this objective. The analysis establishes scatter invariances, conditional local descent, and coefficient bounds, and shows why clean geometry alone cannot guarantee input robustness. Six-seed classification comparisons are complemented by a 104-run study with separate control selection and ten-seed evaluations on each of two backbones. At 60% annotations on ImageNet-100, entropy acquisition with DCR and MACC-Lite reaches 73.420 +/- 0.512% accuracy on ResNet-50 and 73.086 +/- 0.397% on ResNet-18 (mean +/- seed SD), exceeding entropy alone by 1.742 and 1.834 percentage points. Both gains survive Holm correction under paired-t and exact sign-flip tests, with unchanged feedback settings across backbones. Independently tuned constants attain slightly higher mean accuracy; the paired comparisons establish neither adaptive superiority nor equivalence. Separate six-seed spectral studies yield a small fixed-regularization C4 loss reduction, worse CIFAR-10 diffusion FID, and inconclusive AudioCaps captioning effects. Earlier classification studies find near-zero AutoAttack accuracy at L-infinity radius 8/255 and no added benefit from learned control. The results support the evaluated combined classification treatment and identify the task dependence of geometry-informed regularization.

- **Comment**: Accepted version to Els Pattern Recognition
- **Journal**: Pattern Recognition, 172:115120, 2026
- **Summary**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) allows for high-quality real-time novel-view synthesis. Since its inception in 2023, its explicit and highly configurable scene representation has been extended toward sparse-view generation, dynamic reconstruction, efficiency and compression, streaming, and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). Although Gaussian Splatting can provide real-time rendering, rendering an existing representation is distinct from constructing, updating, or maintaining the scene itself. - **Summary**: We propose a unified spatial-density signal to score token importance for both inference phases: bipartite merge for prefill and KV eviction for decode, yielding measurable gains in each stage. At 0.125 keep ratio on TextVQA, it surpasses H2O by 9.6 points; InternVL-3.5 models (2B to 38B) maintain a 7 to 10 point advantage. Compression scales favorably with model size: every measured 8B to 38B model transition reduces accuracy loss instead of worsening it. We suggest MM-Vet or DocVQA as mandatory full-pipeline validation for any decode-phase claims. Our code is available at https://github.com/Kai-Liu001/TwinPrune

- **Summary**: Cross-species portrait animation, which aims to animate a source portrait of species A using a driving video of species B, is particularly challenging due to substantial anatomical differences in facial structures across species. - **Summary**: Estimating simulation-ready material parameters from visual observations is crucial for deformable-object manipulation but challenging because material properties are only indirectly revealed through physical responses. - **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) codecs report reconstruction quality after compression, and give users little control over the distortion of individual primitives. We present PLEDGE, a training-free, progressive, error-bounded codec that translates four user-declared tolerances into per-primitive quantization bounds: screen-space position, footprint deformation due to rotation and scale, view-dependent color, and opacity. The four tolerances incur substantially different rate costs, so users can spend rate where their application needs it.

- **Summary**: One-step diffusion distillation achieves strong performance on small datasets but remains insufficient for high-resolution or complex text-to-image settings. Extending to multistep distillation often requires training data, suffers from early-step degradation, inconsistent outputs across sampling budgets, and incurs high computational cost. We identify that these limitations stem from simultaneous training, where a single adapted network approximates multiple score functions of different student distributions at once, causing biased distribution matching and unstable optimization. Therefore, we propose Progressive Multistep Data-free Distillation (PMDD), which assigns a dedicated adapted network per student distribution while avoiding the memory overhead of maintaining multiple adapted networks. Combined with a stage-wise unforget loss to preserve earlier-step generations, PMDD reduces optimization bias in the student, provides faster convergence, and a single unified model that supports multiple inference budgets. Extensive experiments show that PMDD matches or outperforms data-free few-step baselines at every multistep budget, reaches FID 1.99 on ImageNet 64x64 in two steps, below its 512-NFE teacher (2.32), and achieves FID 7.76 on zero-shot COCO 512x512, while requiring 4-5x less training compute than data-free DMDv2 to support three inference budgets.
