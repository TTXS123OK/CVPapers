### AdShot: Benchmarking Multimodal Large Language Models for Video Advertisement Clipping
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33377v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.MM**, cs.CV
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33377v1)

- **Authors**: Wen Xie, Om Rastogi, Sai Siddhartha Vivek Dhir Rangoju, Gijs Overgoor, Yakov Bart, Sarah Ostadabbas
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Advertisers routinely create multiple duration variants of ads to meet marketing budgets and viewer preferences, a labor-intensive and costly process. While multimodal large language models (MLLMs) excel at general video understanding, their ability to perform specialized editorial tasks remains unexplored. We introduce AdShot, a benchmark for evaluating MLLMs on shot selection for ad clipping. It contains 823 professionally edited ad pairs (30-sec sources and 15-sec edits) across 194 brands and 17 industries, annotated with shot-level boundaries and content features (action, emotion, information, imagery). We evaluate 13 state-of-the-art video-only MLLMs and 2 audio-visual MLLMs (7B-72B parameters) on shot selection accuracy (Precision, Recall, F1), duration constraints (15s), and content preservation, including an audio ablation. We find: (1) model architecture matters more than scale, with smaller models often matching or exceeding larger ones; performance is strongest for target ads with 6-10 shots; (2) models exhibit a mean absolute duration error of 5.39s, with most tending to overshoot the 15s target, though they can still substantially reduce human editing effort; (3) all models exhibit systematic content biases, favoring information-rich and emotion-focused shots while under-selecting action and imagery. We publicly release our benchmark to facilitate research at the intersection of multimodal AI and computational advertising.

- **Updated**: 2026-10-24 02:06:09+00:00
- **Authors**: Fan He, Baining Wu, Yan Li, Xiaoyang Zeng
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multi-object tracking in satellite video underpins persistent monitoring, yet offline-tuned trackers lack the onboard intelligence and efficiency to adapt within an observation of tens of seconds. The root is that adaptation still means relabelling and retraining the detector, which cannot run on board in time. Tuning the tracker configuration strongly controls accuracy without touching the network, yet search needs labelled or proxy evaluations, and a language agent costs seconds and tokens per decision. We propose a dual-speed agentic framework pairing a fast instinct compiled offline with a slow reason serving the mission. Its algorithm, Arbitration of Unlabelled Shortlists from Prior Experience, or AUSPEX, learns a covering shortlist and a label-free ranker from labelled source tasks and commits one configuration per sequence. On SatMTB and SatVideoDT with three recent detectors, AUSPEX raises IDF1 by 2.84 to 2.94 points and MOTA by 3.21 to 3.29 points on average without target labels, balances accuracy and labels best among sixteen methods, and loses at most 0.28 IDF1 points at 1.25 tracking passes. CORVUS executes four missions with execution accuracy up to 0.99 and target recall at the perception ceiling, spends no token on the routine decision, runs on Jetson Xavier NX, and hosts fifteen language models from six families without altering any commitment, demonstrating its extensibility and generalization.

- **Summary**: Generative models for microscopy can already produce realistic whole-image fields of view, but offer no principled way to control how each individual cell looks. ### Bidirectional Cross-frequency Domain Interaction Network for UHD Low-Light Image Enhancement

- **Authors**: Bo Fu, Hai Chi, Liyan Wang, Shilin Fu, Wei Wang, Kin-Man Lam, Jinshan Pan, Cong Wang
- **Comment**: 13 pages, 14 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Existing Ultra-High-Definition (UHD) low-light image enhancement (LLIE) methods typically rely on either spatial- or frequency-domain modeling, or independently process different frequency components for efficient restoration. However, they often fail to jointly exploit complementary spatial--frequency information and the inherent relationship between illumination and structural information across frequencies, leading to inconsistent illumination, residual noise, and detail artifacts. In this paper, we propose a novel Bidirectional Cross-frequency Domain Interaction Network (BiFreqNet) for UHD LLIE. Specifically, BiFreqNet incorporates a Dual-domain Feature Interaction (DFI) module into the multi-scale representations of a pretrained variational autoencoder (VAE) to perform reciprocal enhancement between low- and high-frequency features. DFI consists of a Spatial and Spectral-Aware Low-Frequency Enhancement (S$^2$LFE) block and a Cross-Frequency Enhancement (CFE) block. S$^2$LFE exploits high-frequency structural cues to guide low-frequency restoration and jointly models spatial and spectral information to recover global exposure and spatially varying illumination while suppressing sensor noise. CFE further leverages the enhanced low-frequency contextual information to guide adaptive high-frequency refinement, enabling faithful reconstruction of structures and fine details. Extensive experiments on four general and UHD LLIE benchmarks demonstrate that BiFreqNet achieves competitive or superior performance compared with state-of-the-art methods.

- **Summary**: World action models have demonstrated promising performance in robot policy learning, yet their high inference latency remains a critical bottleneck for real-world deployment. Recent approaches have sought to improve inference efficiency by reducing test-time future-frame imagination or accelerating action prediction. However, these methods often leverage representations from all video world model layers without explicitly considering their varying relevance to action prediction, leading to redundant computation and suboptimal representation utilization. In this work, we propose TurboWAM, a fast world action model that efficiently aggregates structured world representations conditioned on action predictions. Specifically, TurboWAM introduces a structured world representation aggregation module that adaptively fuses representations from different world model layers according to their relevance to the evolving action denoising state. Furthermore, TurboWAM employs a structured action supervision mechanism that adaptively modulates gradients from action supervision across different world model layers during backward propagation, facilitating world representation learning. During inference, by reducing the number of world model layers executed and caching relevant representations across action denoising steps, TurboWAM substantially reduces computational overhead. Extensive experiments on multiple robot manipulation benchmarks demonstrate that TurboWAM achieves low inference latency while maintaining competitive task success rates compared with state-of-the-art methods.
